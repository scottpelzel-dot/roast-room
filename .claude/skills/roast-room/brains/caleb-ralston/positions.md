# Positions: Caleb Ralston

Built from notes on all 8 sources (S001 to S008). 40 topics plus a group of general founder topics, most-evidenced first, with founder questions on personal brand and content weighted over general business. Every source is undated. Where he names a time inside a source, it is given as his own reference: S005 mentions February 2025, S004 the back half of 2025 (August 2025), S007 says the course follows his 2025 launch, S006's title names 2026 (he says it was filmed in December 2025), and S008 dates a LinkedIn remark to February 2025. Topics with no evidence say so.

## Positioning: optimize for trust, not virality
- **Stance:** His core contrarian belief. A founder selling through a personal brand should build trust and conversions, not views. Views that do not convert are a cost, not an asset.
- **Quotes:** "I believe that entrepreneurs building a personal brand to grow their business should optimize for trust, not virality." (S002) "But I believe that the northstar that you should be optimizing around is trust and conversions." (S004) "I don't know about you, but I can't pay my bills with views." (S002)
- **Mechanism:** Optimizing for virality widens the topic, so views rise while buyers tune out. "So, as views and followers are going up to the right, guess what sales are doing? Straight down." (S007) "You end up alienating the people that actually want to buy from you." (S006)
- **Limit he states himself:** It assumes a high-ticket offer to a narrow group. For mass-market products he says the opposite: "For those kinds of brands, going viral is a lot more effective." (S006) He is "not saying going viral is bad." (S002)
- **Sources:** S002, S004, S005, S006, S007 (5).

## What a brand is: pairing times consistency
- **Stance:** Brand is not a logo or a vibe. It is the association people form because you pair yourself with the same things over and over. It exists whether you manage it or not.
- **Quotes:** "I define branding as a pairing of things, and I define good branding as an intentional pairing of relevant things done consistently." (S002) "Pairing times consistency equals association." (S007) "your brand actually exists whether you define it or not." (S005) "I believe that your brand is not what you say, it's what you do and prove over and over again." (S005)
- **Caveat he adds:** "Intentional doesn't mean right or good." (S005) You can be consistent with the wrong pairings.
- **Sources:** S001, S002, S004, S005, S007 (5).

## Whether to build a personal brand at all
- **Stance:** Start from the goal, not from content. Work back from the outcome you want (brand journey framework). If public content makes the goal slower or harder, do not make it. Some people should never be known.
- **Quotes:** "does making content accelerate my ability to get there, or does it actually make it slower and harder for me to accomplish that goal?" (S007) "Exercise number one is the brand journey framework. The most important framework for your personal brand." (S004) "Because if you don't have a really good intention behind it, I would argue for a lot of people, it's actually not worth it." (S008)
- **Read:** He credits Gary Vaynerchuk with telling him he might build a great business without content (S007). He uses an executive-protection friend as the case where fame hurts the goal (S007).
- **Sources:** S002, S004, S005, S007, S008 (5).

## What you stand for: the contrarian belief
- **Stance:** The biggest lever for standing out is a view of the industry that differs from your peers. It must be true to you and earned in practice. Contrarian, never controversial for reaction.
- **Quotes:** "I would argue that the most important thing to nail down is your contrarian belief." (S004) "I encourage you build a contrarian belief, not a controversial belief." (S007) "don't develop a contrarian take to have one. It needs to actually be true to what you believe." (S004) "The moment that anybody brings up any sort of push back on it, we're going to crumble" (S003)
- **How he tests it:** Run two or three takes and keep the one the audience quotes back. "Are people commenting literally quoting your contrarian belief back at you?" (S007)
- **Order of levers:** contrarian belief first, delivery second, wrapping paper third (S004, S006, S007).
- **Sources:** S002, S003, S004, S006, S007 (5).

## What you stand against: associations and anti-associations
- **Stance:** Name two things you want to be known for and two you never want tied to you. Use them to say no to guests, shows, topics, tactics and sponsors, even big ones.
- **Quotes:** "Identify two things that, no matter how tempting it is, you never want your personal brand to be associated with." (S002) "Not all press is good press. Not all views are good views." (S004) "brand is just as much about what you choose not to do as what you choose to do" (S001) "It creates more trust and loyalty, not just from the actions we take, but the things that we decide not to do." (S003)
- **His own:** against leading creatives with fear (S004); no famous faces of past bosses in his thumbnails, which he admits costs views (S003).
- **Sources:** S001, S002, S003, S004, S005, S007, S008 (7).

## Trust
- **Stance:** Trust is a forecast from past behavior, and it is what makes people buy. Content exists to scale it.
- **Quotes:** "I believe that trust is how much you believe that someone or something will meet your expectations in the future based on how they have behaved in the past." (S006) "I believe that trust is the currency that precedes the transaction." (S004) "making content online I believe boils down to scaling trust" (S001) "It is way harder to rebuild trust than it is to maintain trust." (S005)
- **How to build it in a piece:** name the painful problem, give your solution, set the expectation, make the action easy, and repeat (S006, S007). The trust chain: show success stories, the audience acts, gets results, and associates you with their own success (S001, S005, S008).
- **Sources:** S001, S002, S004, S005, S006, S007, S008 (7).

## Posting volume: the accordion
- **Stance:** Volume is for learning, not for flooding feeds. Post a lot early to learn what the audience calls quality, then put the same effort into fewer pieces. Expand again when results fade.
- **Quotes:** "But I like to think of volume as data acquisition." (S008) "More volume equals more data on what you want more of from me." (S004) "I like to say reduce the volume and increase the effort per piece that you make." (S008)
- **Shift he names:** In S005 (February 2025 era) he says he is now "on the higher effort per piece of content train right now" and that this is "way different than what I would have" said before (S005). Evidence he gives: one video a month at 1M views reaches more new people than four at 250K (S005).
- **Sources:** S002, S004, S005, S006, S007, S008 (6).

## Consistency, cadence and burnout
- **Stance:** Sustainable beats intense. Start with a cadence that feels easy and raise it, because cutting back later is hard. Most people quit in year one. Expect little for three years.
- **Quotes:** "If it sounds like a push, don't do it." (S006) "Very easy to increase, but once you set an expectation with your audience, a lot of creators struggle to walk that back." (S007) "you're going to beat the creator that tried to sprint hard for a year and then burned out and gave up after 12 months." (S006) "I would recommend you go into building and amplifying this personal brand not expecting any meaningful outcomes for the first 36 months." (S002)
- **Numbers:** Daily posting is unrealistic for most (S005). Pillar content weekly or every two weeks is ideal, monthly is fine (S004). He himself posted one pillar piece a month at the start (S004).
- **Sources:** S002, S004, S005, S006, S007 (5).

## Platforms: how many and which
- **Stance:** Do not be everywhere. Solo: one primary platform (native pillar content, gets the innovation) and one secondary (repurposed). With a team: three primaries. Innovate on one at a time (Eye of Sauron) while the others run in maintenance mode. Never depend on only one.
- **Quotes:** "Trying to be everywhere all at once will cause you more harm than good." (S007) "That is a terrible strategy for you. You are not Gary Vee. You're not Gary Vaynerchuk." (S006) "don't be romantic about the platform just because you already have an audience on Instagram doesn't mean you shouldn't move over to Tik Tok and start to build over there" (S001)
- **Choosing:** Ask someone like your ideal customer where they spend time, then start. "Don't be lazy, just ask." (S004) "If you are complicating it, I'm gonna call you out right now. You are using it as a form of procrastination" (S006)
- **Inconsistency he admits:** He recommends two or three platforms but ran four: "So, there's a little bit of a discrepancy there." (S005, also S001)
- **Sources:** S001, S002, S004, S005, S006, S007, S008 (7).

## LinkedIn (dated view)
- **Stance (February 2025):** The best organic reach of any platform, and his most-converting channel on one team. "There is not another platform that has more true organic reach than LinkedIn." (S008) "LinkedIn is, in my opinion, the hottest platform to be on." (S005)
- **Caveat:** In S005 he calls the reach claim a hypothesis he believes but has not tested, and in S008 ties it to the date. Treat as time-bound.
- **Sources:** S005, S008 (2).

## Content strategy: depth before width
- **Stance:** Most content should go deep on the one thing you want to be known for. Widen only inside your lane. A small personal share is what no one can copy.
- **Quotes:** "You're going to be doing 75% deep content that proves your expertise." (S004) "It's not wide for the world. It's wide for the lane that you are driving in." (S004) "General discoverability is not useful to you or your business." (S004)
- **Ratios differ across sources:** 80/20 core and interests (S005, S008); 75/20/5 deep, niche-wide, personal (S006, S007); by stage 100/0/0, then 75/20/5, then 65/25/10 (S004). He calls them "not a rule" (S006). Read: the later sources (S004, S006, S007) cut the personal share to 5% early, below the 20% in S005 and S008.
- **Sources:** S004, S005, S006, S007, S008 (5).

## Niche
- **Stance:** Start narrower than you think, in the body of water your credibility supports. Widen in layers to adjacent topics once you have expertise and traction. But niching down misread becomes a cage.
- **Quotes:** "Most people never actually stand out because they chose the wrong body of water to compete in." (S003) "A pro tip on this is to expand in layers, not leaps." (S005) "I think people misunderstand niching down" (S004)
- **Tension:** In S008 he grants that a narrow niche grows faster: "There's no debate there." (S008) He still argues for showing more sides of yourself over time.
- **Sources:** S003, S004, S005, S008 (4).

## Content that sells
- **Stance:** Let the content do the selling. Teach the real frameworks, show client results, and answer objections inside the content. Sell the execution, not the knowledge.
- **Quotes:** "I try to share the knowledge and sell the execution." (S008) "I like to let my content do the selling, not me." (S008) "99.9% of businesses are making content online to increase sales" (S001) "don't make art for Art's Sake make creative that converts" (S001)
- **Best case vs reality:** "The best version, and then we can work back to reality, is you never make a right hook in your organic content." (S008) Give in organic, ask in paid (S008). Yet he ends his free course with an application link to his firm and pitches his paid program mid-video (S005, S007).
- **Sources:** S001, S004, S005, S007, S008 (5).

## Educational versus entertainment content
- **Stance:** For a business, educational content wins because it makes viewers act. Many teams think they make education but make entertainment, and leads stall.
- **Quotes:** "It's because they thought they were making educational content when in reality they were making entertainment content." (S006) "Their previous average per video was four to 5,000 leads. This video got over 20,000 leads." (S007) "98% of them come from the lens of entertainment content, not educational." (S006)
- **Sources:** S005, S006, S007 (3).

## When to monetize
- **Stance:** Earn trust before asking. In the first stage, go six to 12 months with no big public asks. If you must sell early, do it one to one and in private.
- **Quotes:** "But the reality is you need to earn trust with your audience before they're going to be willing to transact with you." (S004) "do your darnest to go for six to 12 months without making any big asks of the audience." (S004) "They push offers before trust is built and then they wonder why no one buys." (S005) "The key here is you want to monetize trust, not just attention." (S004)
- **Allowance:** "So, if that is the case, then you got to do what you got to do." (S005)
- **Sources:** S004, S005, S008 (3).

## Giving away your best for free
- **Stance:** Give away what competitors sell. His free 6.5-hour course is his main proof.
- **Quotes:** "Don't do the watered down version, not some teaser of what they can then go and purchase." (S005) "So, putting out this course for free in no way hurt our business." (S004) "It got 1.3 million views, but it has produced over 45,000" (S002)
- **Numbers drift across sources:** 22,000 email subscribers in five months (S004); 44,000 on the list at year end (S007); 45,000 plus leads from the course (S002, S003).
- **Sources:** S002, S003, S004, S005, S007 (5).

## Lead generation
- **Stance:** Capture email so you are not at the mercy of algorithms, using an implementation tool (workbook, worksheet, prompt) that helps people act. He dislikes the term lead magnet.
- **Quotes:** "The marketing world has, in my opinion, kind of ruined the term lead magnet." (S003) "information does not mean implementation" (S003) "so that you're not just beholden to the algorithms of these platforms serving your content, but you have a direct line of access to your customers." (S005)
- **On email:** "Only send shit if you have true actual value." (S008) At the wine company, email beat social for direct sales (S001).
- **Track:** quality of inbound over volume. "The next thing that you want to track is quality of inbound. This is an indicator of trust." (S004)
- **Sources:** S001, S003, S004, S005, S007, S008 (6).

## Offers and pricing
- **Stance:** Start with one offer and refine it. Do not start with high ticket. Ladder from free content to lead magnet, low, mid, then high ticket.
- **Quotes:** "Start with one offer, refine it, and then begin to expand." (S005) "Don't start with five different offers. You're going to confuse your audience." (S005) "Most successful brands don't start with their high ticket offer first." (S005)
- **Price tiers he gives (S005):** low ticket $10 to $100, mid $500 to $5,000, high $10,000 plus. He says this is "the best case scenario." (S005)
- **Tension:** Most of his content advice assumes you already sell high ticket to a narrow group (S006, S007), while his offer advice says not to start there.
- **Pricing itself:** no recorded position on how to set a price or on discounting. Nearest: he was tempted to charge for his course and made it free (S005).
- **Sources:** S005, S006, S007 (3).

## Packaging and wrapping paper
- **Stance:** The idea is most of the result, but the wrapper (title, thumbnail, hook, format, set) earns the click. Do not use your niche's wrappers. Keep a library of packaging from outside your space.
- **Quotes:** "Your rapping is the only competitive advantage you have before somebody clicks." (S006) "I believe the idea is a minimum of 80% of the formula." (S008) "But all this creates is a world of blue M&M's." (S003) "Look outside of your space and niche. Look at creators that are in completely different worlds." (S006)
- **Sources:** S003, S004, S006, S007, S008 (5).

## Hooks and intros
- **Stance:** Short form gives two or three seconds; long form about 15 to 20. Open with a bold or contrarian claim, a pattern interrupt, or a question. In education, give a real learning in the first minute rather than burying the value.
- **Quotes:** "I think you have about 15 to 20 seconds max to capture attention." (S005) "if I learn something in the opening 60 seconds of a video, my belief that I'm going to continue learning throughout the video increases." (S007) "I do think that those are kind of getting a little tiresome at this point." (S005, on contrarian hooks from experts)
- **Method:** the Four C's intro (call out, credibility, compass, core learning) (S006, S007). Copy the intro of your best-retained video, even a low-view one (S004).
- **Sources:** S004, S005, S006, S007 (4).

## Storytelling
- **Stance:** Show traits through stories, not adjectives. Share losses with the lesson. Repeat the same message through new stories.
- **Quotes:** "stories resonate way more with people than you saying typical tropes and attributes like hardworking or innovative or hustling." (S005) "People trust vulnerability more than they trust success." (S005) "The way that you create diversity and newness is the scenarios and stories." (S008) "You can share the same principles through different scenarios over and over and over again." (S004)
- **Structure:** hook, problem, journey (the messy middle), lesson, call to action (S005); a credibility bank of lived lessons (S003, S007).
- **Sources:** S003, S004, S005, S007, S008 (5).

## Repurposing and platform-native content
- **Stance:** Make one pillar piece, mine its self-contained moments, and rewrap each one natively for each platform (waterfall method). Not copy and paste.
- **Quotes:** "This is platform native repackaging." (S006) "self-contained clips. Clips and moments that don't need the context of the surrounding conversation in order to make sense." (S006) "More podcast clips don't do well than podcast clips that do." (S005)
- **Changed view:** "If we were having this conversation two years ago, I would tell you repost all your biggest hitters." (S008) Now: "platforms are actually penalizing an exact reupload" (S008). Yet he still posts the same clip everywhere in his own stage one and calls it a shortcut (S004).
- **Sources:** S004, S005, S006, S007, S008 (5).

## Long form versus short form
- **Stance:** Short form for awareness and reps; long form is where trust and conversion happen. Deep pillar pieces signal leadership.
- **Quotes:** "I view long form as the place where conversion actually happens." (S005) "is mega depth plays. They signal leadership." (S004) "I believe a lot of you should put out a manifesto, a big pillar piece of content that is very extensive that goes extremely in-depth on your views around your core message." (S004)
- **His own year one:** he skipped shorts and focused on YouTube (S007).
- **Sources:** S004, S005, S007, S008 (4).

## Authenticity and delivery
- **Stance:** Delivery is removing what stops you from being yourself, not building a persona. Showing different sides on different platforms is not fake. Be the most useful, not the most charismatic.
- **Quotes:** "Delivery is actually about removing all the things that keep you and are stopping you from being fully you." (S006) "You burn out because you've built a character, not a personal brand." (S006) "You just need to be the most useful. If you optimize your personal brand around usefulness rather than charisma, you will build a strong personal brand." (S006) "What is far more difficult to rip off than expertise is your humanity." (S003)
- **Sources:** S003, S004, S005, S006, S007, S008 (6).

## Founder-led content
- **Stance:** The founder's thumbprint must stay on the content even with a strong team, and the founder should answer comments where possible. Over time, stop being the bottleneck and build other faces so the business does not rest on one person.
- **Quotes:** "But it is my opinion that your thumbrint needs to be on this content." (S004) "I would actually prefer you do that at the detriment of high volume engagement." (S005, on founders replying themselves) "you need to build a system where people are not just here for you, they're here for your team." (S004)
- **Team accounts:** a clearly labeled team account is fine; never pretend to be the founder (S005).
- **Sources:** S004, S005, S007 (3).

## Personal brand versus company brand
- **Stance:** They should complement, not compete. At scale, build other leaders' brands (the bar stool method) to cut key-person risk.
- **Quotes:** "your personal brand and your business are like at odds and competing with each other when really they should be complimentary" (S006) "You need to start thinking the bar stool method." (S004)
- **Sources:** S004, S006 (2). Thin evidence beyond these.

## Content teams and first hires
- **Stance:** Hire around the bottleneck, never to fill an org chart. Platform specialists over generalists. His own first hire was a content director. Do not overhire before content shows a return.
- **Quotes:** "We hire people to solve problems, not to fill an org chart." (S008) "Whatever the bottleneck and constraint is, that is what you want to hire around." (S005) "My first hire in starting my business was not any role." (S004) "Avoid overhiring too early." (S005)
- **Order:** if LinkedIn drives results, hire a LinkedIn writer before a video editor (S005). Stage 2 brings a content director first (S004).
- **Sources:** S001, S004, S005, S007, S008 (5).

## Social media managers and generalist agencies
- **Stance:** Against. Hire channel-specific owners who also make the content.
- **Quotes:** "you want channel specific managers, not a social media manager." (S005) "if they are great at all of the platforms, they suck at all of the platforms." (S005) "I don't hire those because they're a generalist and they don't understand." (S008)
- **Sources:** S004, S005, S008 (3).

## Agencies, contractors or full-time
- **Stance:** Agencies for fast learning, contractors to test a platform cheaply, full-time for daily work, culture and brand consistency. Year one mostly contractors; bring critical roles in-house later.
- **Quotes:** "Agencies are for expedited learning." (S008) "what you could do is take three to six months and test it out with an agency or a contractor." (S005) "They should come in batteries included." (S005, on contractors)
- **Limit:** he admits many cannot pay $15K to $20K a month for one platform agency (S008).
- **Sources:** S005, S008 (2).

## Hiring for culture versus skill
- **Stance:** Culture and mindset over skill, with a paid real-work test for every candidate.
- **Quotes:** "Hire for mindset and train for execution." (S005) "So, I prefer if somebody is technically mid, but they are culturally great." (S005) "Please do a test project before you hire even with a freelancer." (S004) "Make sure you pay fairly for that trial." (S008)
- **Self-critique:** "a lot of the hires that I've made, I actually overemphasize their cultural fit over their technical fit." (S005)
- **Sources:** S004, S005, S007, S008 (4).

## Editing and production quality
- **Stance:** The audience defines quality, not you. Effort goes into what you say, not gear. Post the imperfect piece. But pre-production matters, and he paid for skipping it.
- **Quotes:** "The audience is who determines quality, not you." (S004) "Think more and put more effort into the things that you're saying, not the gear you're using to capture what you're saying." (S004) "I like to say that perfect is the enemy of posted." (S005) "I chose my time over the audience's time." (S007)
- **On sets:** "We don't need another creator with the red and blue light, the fake plant, and the bookshelf full of books that you've never actually read in the background." (S003)
- **Editors:** default to the editor's call and give more notes on what you like (S005, S008).
- **Sources:** S002, S003, S004, S005, S007, S008 (6).

## Metrics
- **Stance:** Track only what changes your actions. Grade each post as a multiple of your 90-day median. Followers are a demand signal, not a KPI. Cross-check platform wins against inbound lead quality. Shares outrank likes.
- **Quotes:** "if it's not changing what you do, it's pointless to track." (S008) "I like to track based on outliers using a multiplier. Establish your 90day average." (S004) "Follower count, in my opinion, is just a signal of demand." (S004) "when you recommend something, you're risking a little bit of your reputation with anybody that you're sharing that to." (S004)
- **Kill rule:** under 1x three times in a row, try something else (S004).
- **Honesty:** "I don't ever wanna get into the weeds of pretending like I know the algorithms and stuff like that." (S008)
- **Sources:** S004, S005, S007, S008 (4).

## Experimentation
- **Stance:** 70% proven, 20% iterations, 10% big swings. Do not punish flops in the 10%, or the team stops testing.
- **Quotes:** "You need that 10%. You need those big swings in order to figure out what the next thing is." (S006) "This is the way that I like to lead media teams to enable psychological safety around testing and experimentation." (S004) "What got you to where you are now will not get you to the next level." (S005)
- **Sources:** S004, S005, S006, S007 (4).

## Audience feedback: comments versus DMs
- **Stance:** For high-ticket offers, steer by customers and private messages, not public comments. Comments are bumpers, not a steering wheel. Let feedback change how you present, never what you believe.
- **Quotes:** "If you want more followers, cool. Listen to your followers. Make the content they're asking you for. But if you want more customers, what are your customers asking for?" (S006) "I view comments DMs as very good bumpers." (S004) "Don't have it change your beliefs. Don't cater what you're saying just to the audience." (S005)
- **Limit:** "this is not a rule, it's an observation." (S006)
- **Sources:** S004, S005, S006, S007 (4).

## Podcast guesting and collaborations
- **Stance:** Go on aligned shows, because each one hands you a new audience. Vet the host first, because you inherit their associations. He turned down a big show that did not fit.
- **Quotes:** "On a podcast, however, you're immediately being presented to a new audience no matter what." (S004) "if you show up on podcast hosted by sketchy, scammy people, then over time your audience is going to associate you with those individuals." (S004) "That was not easy, but it would not put my brand towards where I want to go." (S004) "I try to be very careful about what shows I go on." (S008)
- **Sources:** S001, S004, S005, S008 (4).

## Sponsorships, affiliates and paid ads
- **Stance:** Promote only what you use and what serves your audience. Ads and sponsorships are add-ons, not the model. Paid ads are the right hook after many organic jabs, a stage-3 move; raise organic volume when you add paid.
- **Quotes:** "don't shill out tools and programs or software that you don't actually use." (S005) "This is kind of like a nice to have or an add-on to an already established, more effective and consistent monetization model." (S005) "Your jabs are the organic content that you're producing and putting out there." (S004) "you post and you just give, give, give, give, give in your organic, and then you ask in paid." (S008)
- **Sources:** S004, S005, S008 (3).

## AI in content
- **Stance:** AI makes information a commodity, so being human is the edge. Most AI-written content sounds the same.
- **Quotes:** "99 of people are posting or using chat gbt prompts to make their content, and they all sound like robots and exactly the same." (S008) "I think that is the way to stand out amidst the ever growing amount of AI slop." (S007) "be the one who stands out by not just sharing information, sharing information through a human filter." (S004)
- **Also:** he flags candidates using hidden AI to answer interviews (S005). No recorded position on using AI tools inside a content workflow.
- **Sources:** S004, S005, S007, S008 (4).

## Trends and virality
- **Stance:** Skip trends outside your space; those viewers will not buy. Think in series, not one-hit virals.
- **Quotes:** "Don't follow a trend just because it's a trend." (S005) "But if it's a trending topic that's completely outside of your space and you're just doing it to get views, it's not going to be the right views." (S004) "Rather than thinking about a one-hit viral video, think more like Netflix" (S005)
- **Note:** his TikTok work for Gary was trend-fluent short form; he says TikTok success no longer predicts success elsewhere (S004).
- **Sources:** S004, S005, S006 (3).

## Rebranding and changing a public stance
- **Stance:** What you say early becomes the cage you live in, so do not take stances you would not defend in 10 to 15 years. Changing your mind publicly is admirable but costs you. Evolve without abandoning your roots.
- **Quotes:** "What you say now influences your ability to act later." (S007) "what you can end up doing is actually creating a prison that your brand lives within and cannot escape." (S007) "I admire people that admit that they said one thing one way and they have new information now and they're changing their view on something, right?" (S007) "Nostalgia is a very big winner" (S005)
- **Full rebrand of a company:** no recorded position.
- **Sources:** S004, S005, S007 (3).

## Competition
- **Stance:** Do not copy the leader in your niche; look outside it. Build from customer pain, not competitor moves.
- **Quotes:** "they look at the top dog in their space and they just duplicate everything. And guess what you will always be? Number two, tops."
