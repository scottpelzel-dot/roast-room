# Principles: Caleb Ralston

30 decision rules, built from the four notes batches on all 8 sources (S001 to S008), most-supported first. "Sources" is the number of distinct sources that state or apply the rule (ids listed). Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1. All sources are undated transcripts; where a view changed or conflicts, both sides are shown.

1. **Optimize for trust, not virality, when you sell a high-ticket offer to a narrow group.** "I believe and I preach that you should optimize for trust, not virality." (S007) "I don't know about you, but I can't pay my bills with views." (S002) Sources: 6 (S001, S002, S004, S005, S006, S007). Confidence: high. **Condition he sets himself:** for mass-market, low-ticket products he says going viral works better: "kinds of brands, going viral is a lot more effective." (S007) He is not against going viral, only against optimizing for it (S002).

2. **Don't be everywhere; focus on a few platforms, one at a time, but never depend on just one.** "Trying to be everywhere all at once will cause you more harm than good." (S007) "don't be romantic about the platform just because you already have an audience on Instagram doesn't mean you shouldn't move over to Tik Tok and start to build over there" (S001) Sources: 7 (S001, S002, S004, S005, S006, S007, S008). Confidence: high. **Inconsistency:** he advises one primary and one secondary for solo creators, yet prioritizes four platforms himself in S001 and admits the gap in S005.

3. **Be obsessive about who and what you pair with, and say no to big but misaligned shows, sponsors and collaborations.** "Not all press is good press. Not all views are good views." (S004) "I try to be very careful about what shows I go on." (S008) Sources: 6 (S001, S002, S003, S004, S005, S008). Confidence: high.

4. **The audience decides what quality is; post enough to find out, then judge only by their response.** "Quality is not your personal preference. Quality is determined by your audience. Plain and simple." (S005) "The only people that actually know and determine what quality is is your audience." (S008) Sources: 5 (S002, S004, S005, S006, S008). Confidence: high.

5. **Choose a cadence you can sustain for years, start low and raise it; never start at a push.** "Prioritize the consistency over the perfection." (S004) "If it sounds like a push, don't do it." (S006) Sources: 5 (S002, S004, S005, S006, S007). Confidence: high.

6. **Only build a public brand if it moves you toward a stated goal; work backward from the outcome.** "does making content accelerate my ability to get there, or does it actually make it slower and harder for me to accomplish that goal?" (S007) "Because if you don't have a really good intention behind it, I would argue for a lot of people, it's actually not worth it." (S008) Sources: 5 (S002, S004, S005, S007, S008). Confidence: high.

7. **Pick topics from your ideal customer's painful problem, not from expected views, and take direction from customers over followers.** "You're not picking topics because you think it's going to get the most amount of views, you're picking topics because it's going to solve the biggest pain point for your ideal customer." (S002) "If you want more followers, cool. Listen to your followers. Make the content they're asking you for. But if you want more customers, what are your customers asking for?" (S006) Sources: 5 (S002, S004, S005, S006, S007). Confidence: high. He says high-ticket buyers rarely comment and instead DM or email, and calls that an observation, not a rule (S006, S007).

8. **Be the most useful, not the most charismatic; content should change what the viewer does.** "Whoever is the most useful in your niche will always win." (S003) "You just need to be the most useful. If you optimize your personal brand around usefulness rather than charisma, you will build a strong personal brand." (S006) Sources: 5 (S002, S003, S005, S006, S007). Confidence: high.

9. **Make content native to each platform; don't copy and paste the same file everywhere.** "This is platform native repackaging." (S007) "platforms are actually penalizing an exact reupload" (S008) Sources: 5 (S004, S005, S006, S007, S008). Confidence: high. **View changed:** "If we were having this conversation two years ago, I would tell you repost all your biggest hitters." (S008) He still allows plain repurposing in the first years (S005) and posts the same clip everywhere himself in stage 1 as a shortcut (S004).

10. **Be human; your humanity is the edge that expertise and AI cannot copy.** "What is far more difficult to rip off than expertise is your humanity." (S003) "I think that is the way to stand out amidst the ever growing amount of AI slop." (S007) Sources: 5 (S003, S004, S005, S007, S008). Confidence: high.

11. **Earn trust before you ask for money; early asks should be private and one to one.** "But the reality is you need to earn trust with your audience before they're going to be willing to transact with you." (S004) "They push offers before trust is built and then they wonder why no one buys." (S005) Sources: 4 (S004, S005, S007, S008). Confidence: high. He allows exceptions: "So, if that is the case, then you got to do what you got to do." (S005) In stage 1 he suggests six to 12 months with no big asks (S004).

12. **Hold a contrarian belief that is true to you, never a controversial one made for reaction.** "I encourage you build a contrarian belief, not a controversial belief." (S007) "don't develop a contrarian take to have one. It needs to actually be true to what you believe." (S004) Sources: 4 (S002, S003, S004, S007). Confidence: high.

13. **Start narrow and deep, and widen in layers to adjacent topics; never go wide without depth.** "A pro tip on this is to expand in layers, not leaps." (S005) "General discoverability is not useful to you or your business." (S004) Sources: 4 (S003, S004, S005, S008). Confidence: high. **Tension:** he also warns that niching down is misread and can trap you: "And the reality is, is yes, that will allow you to grow much faster for sure. There's no debate there." (S008) His answer is a narrow core plus personal interests.

14. **Optimize for the group you want more of: conversions and inbound lead quality, not follower counts.** "The group that you optimize around is the group that you will get more of." (S007) "Follower count, in my opinion, is just a signal of demand." (S004) Sources: 4 (S002, S004, S005, S007). Confidence: high.

15. **Give away your best knowledge for free and sell the execution.** "So, here's the formula. Share the knowledge, sell the execution." (S005) "So, putting out this course for free in no way hurt our business." (S004) Sources: 4 (S001, S004, S005, S008). Confidence: high.

16. **Show proof and lived experience, including losses with their lessons.** "The top dogs are talking about what they've done because they've actually done something worth sharing." (S003) "People trust vulnerability more than they trust success." (S005) Sources: 4 (S003, S004, S005, S008). Confidence: high.

17. **Don't pose as the expert you are not yet; share the journey of becoming one.** "Don't pretend and posture as the expert." (S007) "you're not the master yet, So share the journey of how you're becoming the master." (S008) Sources: 4 (S004, S005, S007, S008). Confidence: high.

18. **Don't copy the top creator in your space; look outside your niche for ideas and packaging.** "they look at the top dog in their space and they just duplicate everything. And guess what you will always be? Number two, tops." (S008) "Look outside of your space and niche. Look at creators that are in completely different worlds." (S006) Sources: 4 (S003, S006, S007, S008). Confidence: high. He allows copying at the start if you move fast to your own version (S003).

19. **Keep a budget for experiments and make flops safe, because what worked will stop working.** "But if you never test new things, you will certainly plateau." (S005) "you're always trying to put your content out of business." (S007) Sources: 4 (S004, S005, S006, S007). Confidence: high.

20. **Play a long game: expect little for years and outlast the people who sprint and quit.** "I would recommend you go into building and amplifying this personal brand not expecting any meaningful outcomes for the first 36 months." (S002) "it truly is a marathon, not a sprint" (S006) Sources: 4 (S002, S005, S006, S007). Confidence: high. He admits he popped early himself and makes no promises on timing (S002).

21. **Hire to solve a bottleneck, not to fill a role.** "We hire people to solve problems, not to fill an org chart." (S008) "You hire to solve problems. And the problems are the bottlenecks in your process." (S004) Sources: 3 (S004, S005, S008). Confidence: high.

22. **Track only the few metrics that would change what you do.** "if it's not changing what you do, it's pointless to track." (S008) "is if it doesn't change your actions, don't track it." (S005) Sources: 3 (S004, S005, S008). Confidence: high.

23. **Test every creative hire on a real, paid task before you commit.** "Please do a test project before you hire even with a freelancer." (S004) "Make sure you pay fairly for that trial." (S008) Sources: 3 (S004, S005, S008). Confidence: high.

24. **The founder keeps a thumbprint on the content, and writes it in the early stage.** "But it is my opinion that your thumbrint needs to be on this content." (S004) "As the founder, I recommend in this stage you are heavily involved in the content and" (S005) Sources: 3 (S004, S005, S007). Confidence: high. He also says the founder must stop being the bottleneck by stage 3 (S004).

25. **Hire platform specialists, not a generalist social media manager.** "you want channel specific managers, not a social media manager." (S005) "I don't hire those because they're a generalist and they don't understand." (S008) Sources: 2 (S005, S008). Confidence: medium.

26. **What you say in public now limits what you can do later, so protect your future self.** "What you say now influences your ability to act later." (S007) "You don't want to build a personal brand that holds you captive. You want to build a personal brand that provides freedom." (S004) Sources: 2 (S004, S007). Confidence: medium.

27. **Make creative that converts; do art for its own sake on your own time.** "don't make art for Art's Sake make creative that converts" (S001) "If we're making art, we're doing that on our own time as passion projects. This is creative that converts, and that's what we want to be building." (S008) Sources: 2 (S001, S008). Confidence: medium.

28. **Hire for mindset and culture, and train the skills.** "Hire for mindset and train for execution." (S005) "So, I prefer if somebody is technically mid, but they are culturally great." (S005) Sources: 2 (S005, S008). Confidence: medium. He admits the bias: "a lot of the hires that I've made, I actually overemphasize their cultural fit over their technical fit." (S005)

29. **When you are wrong, admit it first and fast.** "When you're wrong, be the first to admit it." (S008) "Just to own it when you're wrong." (S005) Sources: 2 (S005, S008). Confidence: medium.

30. **Don't batch more than about a month of content, so what you learn feeds the next batch.** "I would recommend that you don't do any more than a month at a time." (S004) Sources: 1 (S004). Confidence: low. He says he does not love a month either (S004).
