# Stories: Caleb Ralston

Built from notes on all 8 sources (S001 to S008). 18 stories and cases he reuses, with the lesson he draws. Most-used first. Source ids in parentheses.

1. **The free 6.5-hour course.** In 2025 he posted a 6 hour 22 minute personal brand course on YouTube for free, starting with no email list, and refused advice to split it into 13 videos. He reports 22,000 subscribers in five months, then 44,000 by year end, 45,000 plus leads, and hundreds of applications to work with him. He was tempted to charge for it. Lesson: give away what competitors sell; depth builds trust and feeds the business (S002, S003, S004, S005, S007).
2. **Gary's TikTok.** As an editor at Gary Vaynerchuk's company he volunteered to run TikTok, spent a week scrolling it eight hours a day before posting, then edited about seven clips and posted ten a day. He cites growing it from 300K to 3.5M followers in three months. Lesson: owning one platform end to end, as both creator and manager, is how you learn what good looks like (S004, S005).
3. **Crush It at 15 and the bodybuilders.** A friend gave him Crush It at 15. He filmed his dad's gym, then pitched pro bodybuilders backstage by telling them their video was weak; Flex Lewis said yes. Lesson: you have not because you ask not, and his drive for the subject is why he never has to hype himself up to film (S001, S004, S005).
4. **The cannabis clients and the fired friends.** He tripled his income with two legal cannabis clients and hired three friends, then one client halved his pay overnight and he had to let them go. He made it his first YouTube video. Lessons: never stop finding clients, and sharing a failure with its lesson builds trust (S001, S005).
5. **Gary told him he might not need content.** When he planned his own brand, Gary asked whether content fit the life he wanted. That talk gave him two goals, grow the business and teach people for free, which now filter every post. Lesson: start from the goal; content is optional (S007).
6. **Entertainment posing as education.** A client with a skilled media team grew views but not leads. After retooling toward real education, one video drew over 20,000 leads against a usual 4,000 to 5,000 and became a top-three video of the year. Lesson: educational content converts; entertainment only attracts (S006, S007).
7. **Turning down the big podcast.** He declined a large business show with a host of millions because it did not fit the associations he wants, and he researched a host's past guests before agreeing to another show. Lesson: guest spots pair you with the host; not all views are good views (S004, S005, S008).
8. **Nike and Michael Jordan.** Jordan wore Nikes every game, not now and then, so the pairing became an association. He uses Harley shirts on himself the same way. Lesson: consistency turns pairing into brand (S001, S002, S004).
9. **The viral tweet rewrapped.** A client's tweet took off and became a photographed quote on Instagram, a short using the quote as a hook, a LinkedIn post, and then a long YouTube video, each a top performer. Lesson: prove an idea on one platform, then rebuild it natively on the next (S005, S008).
10. **LinkedIn converted best.** On one team YouTube brought the most leads, but LinkedIn converted at the highest rate, so they moved roughly $20K to $30K a month into LinkedIn organic. For his own firm he hired a LinkedIn writer before a video editor. Lesson: put money where conversions are, not where volume is (S005).
11. **Codie Sanchez and Gary on Facebook.** Codie Sanchez's boring businesses over tech startups, and Gary pushing brands onto Facebook before big brands did, are his standard contrarian-belief examples. Lesson: a true contrarian view is the strongest way to stand out, even in a crowded space (S002, S004).
12. **The 89% intro.** His lowest-view video had his best retention, 89% at 30 seconds, so he copied its intro for the next one. Lesson: learn from the data even in a flop (S004).
13. **The unprepared August shoot.** Filming went badly after an hour because he had skipped pre-production. He posted a 1.5-minute video admitting he chose his time over the audience's, kept the cadence, and went back to heavy prep. Lesson: own the miss publicly and protect the audience's time (S007).
14. **The agency that posted flops.** An agency kept posting weeks of batched videos that clearly failed because they had already been made. Lesson: batch no more than about a month so learning can feed the next batch (S004).
15. **The 2018 fan account.** Gary wanted a smaller same-audience account posting at high volume; posts that hit 1.5x to 2x moved to the main page. Lesson: test cheaply before risking the main channel (S004).
16. **The canceled edit.** An editor spent five to six days at 13 hours a day on a piece the talent disliked. He canceled the upload and explained why to the editor. Lesson: honoring the talent's veto builds the trust that makes them open up on camera (S005).
17. **Trevor and Jason, culture over skill.** He rates Trevor Odum about 6 of 10 as an editor at hire but 10 of 10 on culture, and Trevor rose to lead editor managing three people; Jason Leva's tryout was not top 10 but he took harsh feedback well and became one of his best hires. Lesson: hire for mindset and train for execution (S005).
18. **Brian in the puddle.** A 23-year-old who lost 57 pounds should start with men in their early 20s losing 20 pounds, not the weight-loss ocean. Lesson: your credibility decides the body of water you compete in (S003).
