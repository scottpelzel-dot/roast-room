# Frameworks: Kane Kallaway

Built from extract notes on 33 of his own YouTube videos (S001, S003 to S034; S002 is excluded as a translated transcript). 25 frameworks, most-used first. "Sources" counts distinct sources where the idea is taught or applied (ids listed). Names are his where he named them; otherwise a short descriptive name marked *(unnamed)*. The How-to-score lines are scoring guidance for the council built from his stated thresholds, not quotes. All sources are undated except S020, a 2025 video by its title. S006 (sponsored by Anthropic) and the Sandcastles pitches in S008, S009 and elsewhere are not used as evidence of his positions.

---

## 1. Contrast (A versus B) and the curiosity loop
- **What it is:** His root idea. Curiosity is the gap between what the viewer already believes (A) and what you show them (B). The hook opens a loop with that gap; the body closes it and opens the next. He calls contrast the engine under every other storytelling tool.
- **Parts:** State or imply the common belief; deliver the contrarian alternative that solves the pain faster, better or cheaper; use contrast words (but, actually, instead, turns out, yet, therefore) between beats, never "and then"; stated contrast when you need it blunt, implied when the baseline is well known.
- **When to use:** Any hook, intro, title, or story beat that feels flat. If a premise is dull on its own, force contrast into it.
- **How to score:** 5 if the hook names a belief the target viewer actually holds and breaks it with something true and surprising; 3 if there is a twist but the baseline is vague; 1 if it is a plain statement or a list of facts joined by "and then".
- **Quote:** "In the hook, they believe A, we show them B, and the distance between A and B is contrast. The bigger the contrast, the more the curiosity and the deeper the hook." (S032)
- **Sources:** 14 (S001, S003, S008, S012, S013, S014, S015, S016, S018, S022, S024, S026, S032, S033)

## 2. What a hook must do: topic clarity plus on-target curiosity (single subject, single question; interrupt theory)
- **What it is:** A hook has one job: let the right viewer opt in. It does that by making the topic unmistakable and planting one question this viewer wants answered. In S023 he frames the same idea as interrupt theory: cause a freeze point by answering what the video is about and what the viewer gets for staying.
- **Parts:** One subject; one question planted in every viewer's head; no assumed prior knowledge; plain words, active voice; the right viewer self-selects in and the wrong one leaves.
- **When to use:** Writing or judging the first one to three lines of any short video, ad, or YouTube intro.
- **How to score:** Read the hook alone. 5 if a stranger can name the subject and the one question it plants; 3 if the subject is clear but the question is weak or not tied to the buyer; 1 if it is an opaque teaser ("wait till you see this") or could be read two ways. His rule: if you cannot name the question, rewrite.
- **Quote:** "Hooks really only have one job: to help a viewer decide to opt in and continue watching the video." (S014)
- **Sources:** 9 (S001, S008, S011, S014, S016, S023, S026, S032, S034)

## 3. Triple hook alignment (visual, spoken, text; plus caption and audio)
- **What it is:** A hook has three layers that must mean the same thing: what is shown, what is said, and the words on screen. He says he pioneered the idea. S023 adds the caption's first line; S032 adds audio. Viewers take in a video as a sandwich: visual, then audio, then back to the visual to confirm.
- **Parts:** Visual hook; spoken hook; text hook (title text, not captions); audit that each could be guessed from the others.
- **When to use:** Any short video, and especially when a founder works only on the spoken line. He says the business owners he coaches nearly all make that mistake.
- **How to score:** 5 if all three layers say one thing and the text is three to five words, two lines at most, up for about three seconds; 3 if two layers agree; 1 if there is no text hook or the visual is unrelated to the line.
- **Quote:** "All three of those hooks, text, verbal, spoken, they must be aligned." (S023)
- **Sources:** 7 (S001, S008, S011, S023, S026, S030, S032)
- **Conflict:** He ranks the layers differently across videos. S023 says text hooks are "the most important hook component by far" while S032 says the key visual is the most important part, and S008 puts visual hooks at 100 times a spoken hook (S001 says ten times). Read it as: never skip any layer; for a founder on camera with no striking footage, text and spoken carry most of the weight (S023); when a striking visual exists, build the hook around it (S032).

## 4. Speed to value
- **What it is:** Attention falls off a cliff in the first seconds, so the topic and the value must arrive almost at once. Cut every line that comes before the topic.
- **Parts:** Name the topic in the first one to two seconds; signal the benefit or pain by the second sentence; frontload the best value; then the body, a re-hook, and more value.
- **When to use:** Editing any script, and diagnosing a retention graph that drops in the first seconds.
- **How to score:** 5 if topic and value land in the first sentence; 3 if they land by line three; 1 if there is a warm-up, a greeting, or setup before the topic. If line 3 is the first mention of the topic, cut lines 1 and 2 (S014).
- **Quote:** "Content is basically a race to see if you can signal value before they lose interest." (S034)
- **Sources:** 8 (S008, S014, S024, S026, S030, S031, S032, S034)
- **Conflict:** His window varies: one to two seconds (S014), about 2.5 seconds (S030), about four seconds (S008), "you only have 5 seconds to make it stick" (S032). Use the tightest one.

## 5. Re-hooking and loop openers (the hourglass)
- **What it is:** The hook flips an attention hourglass once; it runs out mid-video. A re-hook confirms the last point mattered and teases the next as bigger, so there is never dead air between one loop closing and the next opening.
- **Parts:** Close a loop; in the same breath open a new question or new stakes; use a contrast word in the middle of the loop opener; place one at every section change.
- **When to use:** Any script longer than about 20 seconds; any retention graph with a mid-video drop.
- **How to score:** 5 if every section change carries a re-hook and the graph holds flat; 3 if there is one re-hook; 1 if it is "just information" after the hook.
- **Quote:** "A loop opener is a phrase that you drop in your script to flip that hourglass back over and reset the timing." (S015)
- **Sources:** 7 (S003, S015, S018, S024, S031, S033, S034)
- **Conflict on timing:** every 20 to 30 seconds in shorts and 60 to 90 seconds in longs (S015); about 20 to 25 seconds into a short and two to three minutes into a YouTube video, at most two re-hooks in a 60 to 90 second short (S034); a two to five minute tension cadence in long form (S031).

## 6. Audience matching (internet darts; the 200-person sample)
- **What it is:** The algorithm is a matchmaker. It maps each video's topic, shows it to roughly 200 mostly non-followers, then boosts, retries or stops. Many videos on one topic for one avatar teach it who to send the next one to; mixed topics blur the fit and every video suffers.
- **Parts:** One audience avatar; a narrow band of topics; vary formats freely but never the audience; after 30 to 50 videos the algorithm learns the community; audit your last 20 videos.
- **When to use:** A founder who posts about many things, a flop streak, or a pitch to "go broad".
- **How to score:** Run his last-20 audit. 5 if all 20 target the same buyer on the same few topics; 3 if most do; 1 if they are spread. If they match and views are still low, the problem is video quality, not targeting (S029).
- **Quote:** "Now this process of consistently making videos about the same few topics for the same avatar is called audience matching." (S027)
- **Sources:** 7 (S012, S013, S015, S020, S027, S029, S030)

## 7. Outlier research and idea mining (idea Legos; hold two, remix five)
- **What it is:** Ideas are the bottleneck, and data beats gut. Find videos that beat their channel's average by a wide margin, break them into parts, keep the parts that won, and remix the rest.
- **Parts:** Outlier score (a video's views divided by the channel's average); a watch list of 20 to 30 top creators in the niche; seven idea Legos (topic, angle, hook structure, story structure, visual format, key visuals, audio); hold the winning pieces, reshuffle the others; study your own winners first, then small and midsize creators in your format.
- **When to use:** Choosing topics, hooks, or formats; any time a founder plans to "just be creative".
- **How to score:** His filters as guidance: outlier score 5x and up, engagement 5% and up to screen out paid deals, 7 to 14 days for news and 3 to 12 months for evergreen (S025). Score 5 if the idea is modeled on a validated outlier with a new angle; 3 if validated but copied flat; 1 if pure gut. Goal: lift hook hit rate from about 1 in 20 toward 1 in 5 (S011).
- **Quote:** "If you make 10 reps based on validated data, you're going to get more winners than 10 reps based on your gut. It's just a fact." (S025)
- **Sources:** 8 (S009, S011, S012, S019, S021, S022, S023, S025)
- **Note:** The click-by-click routine runs on his own product (Sandcastles); judge the method, not the tool.

## 8. Awareness game versus conversion game (pure versus on-target virality)
- **What it is:** Social media is two games. Awareness is broad reach paid by ad views and brand deals. Conversion is on-target reach paid by your own offers. Pick one per channel. For a business, he points to the conversion game.
- **Parts:** Awareness formula: shareworthy, curiosity in the hook, emotional payoff at the end, the largest possible audience. Conversion formula: on-target shareworthy, large on-target audience, problem and solution framed so they cannot be misread, a tactical non-obvious solve usable right away.
- **When to use:** A founder chasing views; deciding whether a viral-looking idea is worth making; judging content by leads.
- **How to score:** For a business channel, 5 if the video is aimed at buyers with a clear pain and a usable solve; 3 if on-topic but broad; 1 if it chases mass views with no path to the offer. Judge a hook by leads, not views (S007).
- **Quote:** "As someone who's played both games, you will get way more dopamine and fun playing the entertainment game, but will make way more money and have way more conversion and stability playing the conversion game." (S012)
- **Sources:** 6 (S007, S012, S016, S024, S027, S029)
- **Tension:** S012 says the awareness game "can crush" and is not evil; S029 calls virality "a trap". Read S029 as aimed at business owners chasing ego views.

## 9. Proof and the trust ladder (inside the six checkpoints)
- **What it is:** After the hook, the viewer runs a fast BS check. Trust comes from a ranked list of evidence, most to least effective. The bigger the ask, the more trust needed; the deeper the pain, the less.
- **Parts:** Trust ladder: proof you solved this for someone like them; proof you did it yourself; a big following or a cosign; evidence you have been in their shoes; common ground; the look and quality of the setup. In S024 it is checkpoint two of six (pain acceptance, trust, plan of attack, likability, holding attention, action).
- **When to use:** Any claim in a hook; any content meant to sell; a founder with no results to show yet.
- **How to score:** His threshold: any one of the top three is enough; without them, stack the bottom three (S024). Score 5 if the hook's claim is backed by concrete numbers within seconds; 3 if backed only by common ground or a polished set; 1 if a bold claim stands alone.
- **Quote:** "Without proof, you're just another person with an opinion. But with proof, you're a source of truth." (S004)
- **Sources:** 6 (S004, S013, S016, S022, S023, S024)

## 10. Comprehension: atomic messaging and saying it twice
- **What it is:** Most viewers do not absorb what is said, and confusion is why they leave. Compress each point to its smallest form, then say it a second time as a metaphor a child would get.
- **Parts:** Sixth-grade vocabulary; fewer words; active voice; restate hard points simply; name concepts; cut any line that is not needed context or a step in the logic.
- **When to use:** Any script, especially from an expert founder who uses jargon.
- **How to score:** 5 if a stranger could repeat the point after one watch; 3 if it needs a second watch; 1 if it uses terms the viewer does not know. Run the script through an AI at sixth-grade level as a check (S014, S034).
- **Quote:** "They don't churn because they don't like you. They churn because the story you start telling stops making sense and they can't follow it." (S011)
- **Sources:** 8 (S005, S011, S014, S016, S017, S024, S031, S034)

## 11. Hook formats and the six power words
- **What it is:** A short list of proven hook shapes, plus the word parts every hook carries. Use a format instead of improvising.
- **Parts:** Six power words (S022): subject, action, objective or end state, contrast, and optional proof and time words. Formats: nine in S026 (secret reveal, case study, comparison, question, education, list, contrarian, personal experience, problem); six archetypes in S032 (fortune teller, experimenter, teacher, magician, investigator, contrarian); about a dozen in S023.
- **When to use:** A blank page, or a hook that does not land.
- **How to score:** 5 if the hook has a clear subject doing an action toward a specific, shocking result, with proof for education content; 3 if the result is vague; 1 if there is no end state. Specific numbers beat vague ones (S023).
- **Quote:** "All great hooks have some subject doing some action." (S022)
- **Sources:** 4 (S022, S023, S026, S032)

## 12. Term branding and story locks
- **What it is:** Six word-level tools that lock attention: term branding (name a concept), embedded truths (state it as already true), thought narration (say what the viewer is thinking), negative frames (warnings beat advice), loop openers, and contrast words. A named idea feels important and becomes a "digital trademark".
- **Parts:** Name your best frameworks, not everything; replace if, maybe, might with when and the reason why; after each major point ask what the viewer is thinking and say it; flip weak points to the negative.
- **When to use:** Polishing a finished script; building a founder's recognizable IP.
- **How to score:** 5 if the core method has a short name and the script carries no hedging; 3 if one is missing; 1 if the method is unnamed and the script is full of "might".
- **Quote:** "Term branding is when you take a concept in your video and give it a name." (S015)
- **Sources:** 4 (S015, S016, S026, S034)

## 13. Lock-in zone and click confirmation
- **What it is:** The one to four sentences after the hook decide whether attention holds. They must confirm the hook or title claim (no bait and switch) and pass a rapid trust check.
- **Parts:** Six lock-in methods (S016): tease the proof, tease a transformation, rapid contrast, the magic box (a named method explained later), objection handling, bold claim double down. On YouTube, the first 10 to 20 seconds confirm the click and ideally beat it (S013, S033).
- **When to use:** A hook that gets views but loses people by second ten.
- **How to score:** 5 if the next lines confirm the promise and add real proof; 3 if they confirm but add nothing; 1 if they drift from the hook's promise.
- **Quote:** "it turns out what you say directly after your hook is the real reason why your videos hold attention." (S016)
- **Sources:** 4 (S013, S016, S023, S033)

## 14. Value checklist: relevant, non-obvious, proven, usable *(unnamed; his lists overlap)*
- **What it is:** The same four or five tests recur under different names: five universal inputs (S004), four content shifts (S005), four horsemen of engagement (S027), and the conversion formula (S012).
- **Parts:** Relevant and narrow to the ideal viewer; small effort for a big reward; a non-obvious frame; validated with proof; tactically usable right away; high absorption. S005 weights direction 30%, comprehension 40%, effectiveness 20%, likability 10%.
- **When to use:** Deciding whether a video idea is worth making for a business channel.
- **How to score:** One point per test passed, out of five. He says the same factors turn viewers into buyers (S027).
- **Quote:** "Relevant and relatable to the viewer. Small effort, big reward, non-obvious frame, validated with proof, and tactically implementable action steps." (S004)
- **Sources:** 4 (S004, S005, S012, S027)

## 15. The desire-based hook
- **What it is:** Tease a dream outcome the viewer already wants, framed by a relatable character with no unfair advantage. He says if he could use only one hook to build a business, this is it.
- **Parts:** Relatable character (the creator, the viewer, or a third party); dream outcome; minimal conditions; executions such as About Me, If I, To You, Can You.
- **When to use:** Content meant to drive leads and sales.
- **How to score:** 5 if a buyer sees themselves and their outcome in the first two lines with no condition they cannot meet; 3 if the outcome is clear but the character feels out of reach; 1 if it leads with a feature or a constraint (like a high price).
- **Quote:** "The goal is to hook the viewer by teasing a desire they already have, framed by a relatable character that they see themselves in." (S007)
- **Sources:** 4 (S007, S010, S013, S016)
- **Conflict:** S007 says the desire hooks more than the problem, but S015 says people are about twice as motivated to avoid pain, and S024 opens with pain acceptance. He holds both: use desire for sales hooks, use a negative frame when a point does not hit.

## 16. Story structures and the modern arc
- **What it is:** Pick a proven structure that fits the topic and your strengths. The school story arc fails online because people scroll before the rising action; his modern arc starts hot.
- **Parts:** Seven structures in S026 (breakdown, newscaster, case study, listicle, problem solver, tutorial, personal story); twelve in S030; his arc in S031 starts at about 70 of 100 intensity, spikes to about 90 early, releases, and rebuilds; reorder the W's to what and why first, then who and how, then when and where.
- **When to use:** Outlining any video longer than a single tip.
- **How to score:** 5 if the structure matches the topic and opens at high intensity; 1 if it opens with background or context before the point.
- **Quote:** "There are seven winning story structures that tend to work across every niche." (S026)
- **Sources:** 4 (S016, S026, S030, S031)
- **Note:** He says the storytelling meta changes every three to six months (S030), so treat any ranking of formats as temporary.

## 17. Content minutes and bingeability (series)
- **What it is:** A stranger needs a certain number of minutes with you before they follow or buy. A series links videos so one view becomes many.
- **Parts:** Hard series (stated number of episodes) or soft series (shared theme and vibe); four to five content minutes for a follow; long form, live and shopping for turning fans into buyers.
- **When to use:** A founder getting views but no follows; planning a content calendar.
- **How to score:** His guidance: four to five content minutes to earn a follow in most niches (S030); a premium brand aims for 30, 60 or 90 plus minutes a week from one viewer (S019).
- **Quote:** "To get a follow in most niches, this only requires four to five content minutes consumed." (S030)
- **Sources:** 4 (S015, S019, S028, S030)

## 18. Premium personal brand and the creator moat
- **What it is:** In an AI flood, information is copied at once, so the moat is a brand that is one of one. You need at least one of five pillars; durable brands stack two to four.
- **Parts:** Five pillars (S019): premium packaging, originality, tactical usefulness, rare, aura. Creator moat (S021): topic niche, visual format, storytelling style, personality and vibe, X factor. Category fandom (S005): be first in one topic folder, not famous overall. Signature visual style (S030): look, set, shot framing, editing.
- **When to use:** A founder deciding whether to be the face, and how to stand out in a crowded niche.
- **How to score:** Count the pillars the founder truly owns: 0 fails, 1 survives, 2 to 4 is durable (S019).
- **Quote:** "I call these the five pillars of premium brand positioning. You must have at least one of these to survive in the long term." (S019)
- **Sources:** 4 (S005, S019, S021, S030)

## 19. Thumbnail and title psychology (packaging)
- **What it is:** On YouTube the click decides the game. The viewer goes thumbnail, to title, back to thumbnail in one to two seconds.
- **Parts:** Visual stun gun, title value hunting, visual validation; a desire loop (education) or interest loop (entertainment) in the title; three elements max; do not repeat the title in the thumbnail text; test at least three designs.
- **When to use:** Any YouTube video, before it is made. Short form has no packaging (S013).
- **How to score:** Best-in-class click-through is about 10 to 12% (S013). Score 5 if title and thumbnail state a dream outcome or pain, use three elements or fewer, and at least three versions are tested; 1 if one thumbnail was made in the last hour.
- **Quote:** "So, the psychology flow is thumbnail to title back to thumbnail." (S010)
- **Sources:** 3 (S010, S013, S033)
- **Conflict:** S013 says do not start the video until title and thumbnail are "relatively dialed"; S033 disagrees with experts who demand both first, saying the title is a must and a loose thumbnail idea is fine.

## 20. The quality ladder (7 out of 10) and quantity versus quality
- **What it is:** Beginners need reps, so start with volume while learning between reps. Never publish below 7 out of 10. Once you can hit 7 reliably, quality wins as the baseline rises.
- **Parts:** The learning era; 7 out of 10 means top four of ten videos on the same topic; push to daily at 7; then slow down to reach 9 and rebuild volume.
- **When to use:** A founder asking "post more or post better?"
- **How to score:** Line up ten videos on the same topic. Below seventh, do not post (S021).
- **Quote:** "Do not dip below 7 out of 10 quality cuz as we've mentioned, anything below that will fail immediately." (S021)
- **Sources:** 4 (S013, S019, S021, S030)
- **Changed view:** He says he has "flip-flopped on this several times" (S021): quality first when he started, then quantity first, now a shift back to quality first because supply is infinite (S019, S021).

## 21. Law of interesting and the four-step script blueprint
- **What it is:** Interestingness is shock value: the distance between what someone knows and what you tell them. Settle the facts before writing, or you optimize a boring concept.
- **Parts:** Curate shocking true facts; pick and write the hook; pick a story structure; filter for the emotion you want at the end. Related: new information or new delivery (S030); three axes of difference: what you say, how you say it, how you explain it (S013).
- **When to use:** Before scripting any video; judging whether a topic deserves a video.
- **How to score:** Score each fact 1 to 100 by how many of 100 people would not know it; keep the 5 to 10 highest (S026). No new information and no new delivery means do not make it (S030).
- **Quote:** "Now, shock value is the distance between what someone already knows about a topic and what you tell them in the video." (S026)
- **Sources:** 3 (S013, S026, S030)

## 22. The dopamine ladder
- **What it is:** Six levels a viewer climbs, each with more reward. Levels one to four are about the message; five and six are about the messenger.
- **Parts:** Stimulation (first one to two seconds of color, motion, contrast); captivation (a question); anticipation (the viewer guesses); validation (a non-obvious answer); affection (they like you); revelation (your name alone pulls them in).
- **When to use:** Reverse-engineering why content works; judging faceless content, which struggles at levels five and six.
- **How to score:** Count how many levels the video reaches. Faceless or pure entertainment rarely reaches six (S003).
- **Quote:** "The highest level of dopamine is achieved just before the answer comes." (S003)
- **Sources:** 2 (S003, S030)

## 23. The addiction loop (stakes, big question, head fake, re-hook)
- **What it is:** Dopamine is about prediction, so a story holds by keeping a prediction open. Give enough to guess, then break the guess with a twist that makes sense at once.
- **Parts:** Stakes (character, something at risk, urgency); a big question loaded early; a head fake; a re-hook as each loop closes. He applies it to client stories, emails and sales calls too.
- **When to use:** Founder stories, case studies, sales calls.
- **How to score:** 5 if stakes are named before the story and the twist is surprising but logical; 1 if it relies on "something crazy happened".
- **Quote:** "The stakes, the big question, the head fake, and the re-hook." (S018)
- **Sources:** 2 (S018, S031)

## 24. TV network strategy (variety show to TV network)
- **What it is:** Feeds now match topics, not followers, so a channel is one show with one repeatable episode type. A brand runs several channels as a network: a front door, a secret R&D account where every post is a test, and spin-out channels for formats that work.
- **Parts:** R&D account; spin out a format once it works at least three times; social architect and social scientist roles; in-house creators.
- **When to use:** A company (not a solo creator) planning content at scale.
- **How to score:** A spin-out channel succeeds if one in five videos pops, not by followers (S028).
- **Quote:** "The single hero channel with multiple different content types does not work anymore." (S020)
- **Sources:** 2 (S020, S028)
- **Note:** S020 (a 2025 video) and S028 are partly predictions; he says much of S028 is "very future forward".

## 25. Hero platform plus email (islands, not ecosystems)
- **What it is:** Win one platform before adding others. Treat each platform as its own island with native content; the only ramp to build is from rented (platforms) to owned (email, community, products).
- **Parts:** Pick the platform where most customers live (ask a random sample of them); add email; stay six months; do not post links that push people off a platform; value pools at the offer, not the media layer.
- **When to use:** A founder told to "be everywhere" or to repurpose to every app.
- **How to score:** 5 if one platform plus email, with a path to an owned offer; 1 if spread thin across five apps with links in every post.
- **Quote:** "Pick the one hero platform where the highest density of your customers live, plus add email, and then only play on that combination for 6 months." (S029)
- **Sources:** 3 (S013, S019, S029)
