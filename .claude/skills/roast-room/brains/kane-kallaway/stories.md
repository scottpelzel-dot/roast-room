# Stories: Kane Kallaway

Built from the batch 1 to 4 extract notes (S001, S003 to S034; S002 excluded). 20 stories and cases he reuses, most-used first, with the lesson he draws. Clips of other creators he plays are not his words; where he retells another creator's result, the lesson is his reading. Source ids in parentheses; all sources are undated.

1. **0 to 100K YouTube subscribers in 5 months.** His second YouTube channel grew from zero to 100K in five months by staying on one audience, putting packaging first, and posting weekly. He uses it as proof that fast growth is possible and as his base hook example ("If I had to grow from zero to 100K subs on YouTube again, here's how I would do it." (S022)). He admits luck: two near-viral videos back to back around his 25th long-form video. "I was planning on not even having one, but I got lucky that I had two back toback." (S013) Lesson: precision plus packaging plus reps, and expect no outliers (S004, S010, S013, S022).

2. **The dead first channel, and the same video at 1,700 views.** His first channel (it had an interview with Mark Zuckerberg) mixed tech videos, vlogs and breakdowns and died. A video that hit about a million views on his new channel had capped at 1,700 on the old one. "Same video, wrong avatar" (S013) In S010 he tells a matching story (1.2 million versus 1,700) and credits the thumbnail change instead, saying it drove six figures in revenue. Lesson: he reads the same gap as audience in one video and packaging in another; both point to the setup around the video, not the video itself (S010, S013).

3. **Life-size floor plans, about 15 million views.** People walking a full-size floor plan in a warehouse, with three to five words of title text and a fortune-teller hook about the future of home design. The visual carried the contrast: "because the fact that it existed on its own was already contrast enough" (S032) Lesson: a key visual and aligned text and spoken hook beat clever words. A related Zuru home-design video with aligned hooks did about 2 million (S011, S016) (S008, S022, S032).

4. **The video he should have canned.** A future-of-storytelling video stalled at about 100,000 views or less: abstract topic, weak b-roll, jargon nobody knew. "what I should have done is probably canned the idea not made it in general" (S032) Lesson: no key visual and no clarity means kill the idea; there are always more ideas (S032).

5. **The Russian engineer's Iron Man suit, 40 million views in 5 days.** He anchored the script on the engineer's claim of a personal hydrogen reactor, which he believed at zero percent. Lesson: interestingness is the distance between what the viewer believes and what you claim (his shock score) (S026).

6. **The Vegas Sphere, 8 million views.** He used it to show his three-step hook: the screen is huge, but the screen is the least impressive part, because the audio is. Lesson: lean the viewer one way, then snap them back (S008).

7. **Desire-hook leads from other creators.** Shot by Sammy (about 2,000 and 1,000 leads), Cassie Schoonover (1,600 and 3,300 leads from one 45-second video) and Minolee (5,000 to 6,000 leads, run eight times for 24 million views). Lesson: a relatable character plus a dream outcome turns views into leads; judge hooks by leads, not views (S007).

8. **The Claude CTA video and the 4x lift.** His hook alone (Claude can optimize your CTAs) needed a second line with proof: "I ran this one time and it increased my leads per video by 4x." (S016) Lesson: a bold claim needs proof in the lock-in zone, or it gets dismissed. He also notes a desire-hook video with no hard proof still did a couple hundred thousand views on a transformation line, but says proof would have been better (S016).

9. **YouTube only after short form.** "I actually didn't start making YouTube videos until I had half a million followers on Instagram and Tik Tok" (S024) He then grew to 65,000 subscribers in about two months, which he credits to beating viewer expectations. Conflict: in S019 he says his marketing brand posted only on YouTube for 18 months before adding Instagram and TikTok. Lesson he draws in S024: build credibility cheaply in short form before paying about $1,000 per YouTube video (S019, S024).

10. **The $52 AdSense moment.** A creator grinds for 12 months, turns on monetization and earns $52, then a $500 and $1,000 brand deal. "$52? Is that it? That's all I'm making from YouTube AdSense?" (S029) His own AdSense is about $3K a month on 400,000 monthly views (S013). Lesson: value does not pool at the media layer; funnel attention to owned products (S013, S029).

11. **Chasing a niche for money.** Early on he picked a niche for the money and it worked for 6 to 12 months before cracking. "That logic works for the first 6 to 12 months. Trust me, I've been there." (S019) Lesson: only obsession with the topic lasts (S019).

12. **Quality first, then quantity, then quality again.** He started as a perfectionist at three videos a week, later studied it like a scientist and moved to quantity, and judged in 2023 and 2024 that two 9s beat one 10. Now he says quality first has returned. "So, that was the narrative I told myself." (S021) Lesson: the right mix depends on how much good content the feed already has (S021).

13. **Taylor Swift at the Super Bowl.** Common angles were her outfit and arrival; he took the business impact on the NFL and pulled a million views. Lesson: the lens, not the topic, sets you apart (S001).

14. **The Stanley cup.** A product nearly discontinued in 2019 that mom bloggers revived with an affiliate deal; his video on it stacks four but-then conflicts in the first 30 seconds. Lesson: stack conflict and context to keep a story moving (S001).

15. **Seven thumbnails for one video.** For the 0 to 100K video his team made seven designs and ran the best three in YouTube's native test. Lesson: even when sure, let the market decide (S010).

16. **The prince, the dragon and the tower, told twice.** Flat, the story made the listener feel nothing; with stakes, a big question, a head fake (the dragon is a prisoner) and a re-hook, the same plot held. In S034 a prince story that wanders into horses and stables shows how one tangent breaks the spell. Lesson: structure, not plot, holds attention, and every line must earn its place (S018, S034).

17. **MrBeast in the pyramids.** The title promises 100 hours inside the pyramids; the first sentence confirms it and raises it with unprecedented access. Lesson: confirm the click, then beat the expectation (S033).

18. **Hormozi's value equation and 1,000 true fans.** Hormozi named his idea instead of calling it a way to improve your offer, and Kevin Kelly's 1,000 true fans has lasted decades. Lesson: named ideas stick; brand your best frameworks (S015).

19. **Series and signature styles of other creators.** Chloe Chia's 30 lessons before 30 took her to about a million followers in four months; Alex Earl grew with a plain room and no editing; JB Copeland cut through with familiar mindset tips in a warm set and slow delivery; Muso made a fisheye bedroom-corner shot his signature. "That is how he cut through, same messages, broadly, different delivery." (S030) Lesson: a series, a look or a new delivery can carry a channel (S030).

20. **The introvert and former rapper.** He calls himself introverted and monotone, raises his energy about 50 percent, and records one line at a time instead of using a teleprompter. "Now, I used to be a rapper, and so that flow is super natural sounding to me." (S017) Lesson: delivery is a learned skill, and rhythm is his edge (S001, S005, S017, S024).
