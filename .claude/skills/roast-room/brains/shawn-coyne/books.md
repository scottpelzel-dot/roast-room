# Books: Shawn Coyne

One book of his own, plus the books he edited or published that he teaches from. Built from the extract notes on 12 sources (S001 to S024). Book text was not fetched; every summary is in my own words, from how he describes the books in the sources.

## The Story Grid: What Good Editors Know (2015)
- **Core thesis:** A story either works or it does not, and an editor can say why. Structure is learnable craft built on thousands of years of storytelling (Aristotle, McKee, Polti and others), so a writer can learn to be their own third-party editor instead of waiting for the muse or a publisher's editor. He calls it an owner's manual for the editorial process and says much of it a given writer may never need (S001).
- **Key frameworks:** genre as the reader's promise and the genre clover; conventions and obligatory scenes; the editor's six core questions; story values, the scene turn and the negation of the negation; beginning hook, middle build and ending payoff; the five commandments of storytelling; the controlling idea; the Foolscap one-page plan; the Story Grid spreadsheet and graph. He teaches the method on The Silence of the Lambs (S001). Later ideas (the 2019 phere golden rule, the 2022 inciting incident taxonomy) are not in the book (S016, S007).
- **Timeline:** In December 2014 he planned to publish in early 2015 and was giving much of it away free online (S024). He says he doubted anyone would want a book of spreadsheets and plot points, and wrote it anyway (S020).
- **Taught in sources:** S001, S002, S003, S008, S012, S016, S019, S020, S023 (every craft source draws on it).

## Story Grid masterwork guides (in progress, 2017)

## Books he edited or published and uses as teaching cases
Not his books. Listed because he reasons from them.

- **Gates of Fire, Steven Pressfield (1998).** Historical war novel about Thermopylae, which Shawn edited. It is where the grid began: he told Pressfield he would throw the grid on an early draft (S019, S001), and years later the grid showed Pressfield what he had done without knowing (S016). Shawn calls it the finest historical war novel he knows (S020).
- **The War of Art, Steven Pressfield (about 2002).** Began as a Xeroxed manuscript Pressfield handed him; Shawn published it at Rugged Land and later as the first Black Irish paperback (S019, S020). He reads it as a big idea book with a named enemy (Resistance) in part one and the solution (turning pro) in part two (S020, S023), and he borrows its ideas of Resistance, turning pro and the shadow career, crediting Pressfield (S020).
- **Do the Work, Steven Pressfield.** Published with Seth Godin's Domino Project (S024).
- **The Profession and The Legend of Bagger Vance, Steven Pressfield.** Cases from his editing: The Profession stalled for years on a missing shift in the middle build (S001); Bagger Vance unlocked once Pressfield found the narrator (S002).

## Books he recommends or analyzes most
In his words as summarized here, not his books.

- **Story, Robert McKee.** The best starting point for story analysis, for novels and narrative nonfiction (S024); source of the controlling idea he prefers to theme (S003).
- **The Silence of the Lambs, Thomas Harris.** His main worked example: the end-of-the-line thriller, one protagonist, a third person that eases into Starling's head, scenes built from character roles (S001, S008, S012).
- **Pride and Prejudice, Jane Austen.** The love story masterwork he read about 20 times to find its obligatory scenes (S002).
- **The Tipping Point, Malcolm Gladwell.** His model big idea book: early stated idea, evidence, sidekicks, ironic ending (S023).
- **Others cited as examples:** Gone Girl and The Martian (S008), And Then There Were None and Hills Like White Elephants (S012), The Shining (S016), Ed McBain's short story Eyewitness (S006, S007), Perennial Seller by Ryan Holiday (S023, he says he did not write any of it).
