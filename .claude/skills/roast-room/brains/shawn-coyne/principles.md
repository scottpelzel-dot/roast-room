# Principles: Shawn Coyne

Built from the extract notes on 12 sources (S001 to S024), mostly Story Grid Podcast episodes with Tim Grahl, 2015 to 2022. 20 decision rules, most-supported first. "Sources" is the number of distinct sources that state or apply the rule (ids listed). Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1.

1. **Pick one global genre, say it plainly, and deliver its promise.** Everything else is a bonus. Sell the book on its global genre, not its subplot. "you have to pick one genre and nail it and then everything else over delivers to the audience" (S002) "It’s just someone who’s afraid to pick a genre." (S003) Sources: 6 (S001, S002, S003, S008, S012, S020). Confidence: high.

2. **If a scene does not turn a value, it is not a scene.** The turn is the heart of a story; a scene where nothing is at stake is the problem to fix first. "you have to start one place with a value and end another." (S012) "People who start telling you episodes in their life, where the value shift never turns are the most boring people in the world, right?" (S016) Sources: 5 (S001, S007, S012, S016, S020). Confidence: high.

3. **Structure is learnable craft, not a gift; treat it as blue collar work.** Talent exists but is not the point; study form like any other trade. "It teaches nothing mystical, it teaches blue collar hard work." (S020) "Anybody who applies themselves and does the amount of work necessary can be a very fine writer." (S019) Sources: 4 (S019, S020, S023, S024). Confidence: high.

4. **Fix the problems, not yourself.** A cheesy or failed draft is a list of craft repairs, not a verdict on the writer. "They’re not the problem. The problems are the problem." (S019) "Don’t kill yourself if you write a cheesy scene. So what? Nobody’s going to read it." (S008) Sources: 4 (S008, S012, S019, S023). Confidence: high.

5. **Read your genre obsessively, then study one masterwork deeply.** Patterns come from volume first, then from close reading. "you have to be really, really, really well read." (S012) "analyze the masterworks and you’ll find them" (S002) Sources: 4 (S002, S003, S012, S019). Confidence: high.

6. **Draft first to the end; edit later with the editor hat.** Do not edit while drafting, do not reread yesterday's pages to start the day, and do not apply editorial notes in the idea stage. "Don’t begin your day by going over what you wrote yesterday." (S001) "It’s a great wild riff that’s going to give you a lot of information for the future and where your story is going to go." (S012) Sources: 3 (S001, S003, S012). Confidence: high.

7. **The ending must be surprising and inevitable, and answer the hook.** Settle the inciting incident and the ending together, then fill the middle. "the ending of the story has to be inevitable but surprising" (S001) "There’s no shame in knowing your ending at the beginning." (S008) Sources: 3 (S001, S003, S008). Confidence: high.

8. **Define the problem precisely, then break it into small ones.** A vague mass of pain stalls a writer; a specific problem can be fixed. "Just find your problems and make a list of your problems, go step-by-step and fix them." (S019) "So break down that huge mass of pain into actionable steps that you could use with craft." (S020) Sources: 3 (S003, S019, S020). Confidence: high.

9. **Give specific notes, never vague ones.** He built the Foolscap and the grid so he could point at the problem. "it will be much easier to fix their book than to just say things like, ‘Well, the middle’s a little slow.’ That just doesn’t help." (S008) Sources: 3 (S008, S019, S020). Confidence: high.

10. **Keep it simple: one premise, one protagonist, two people in a scene.** First-time writers jam in plot and cast; he admits he overcomplicates too. "You don’t want to have six characters with six different motivations because it’s too overwhelming for us, for just a normal person." (S012) "My recommendation is to start very simply." (S003) Sources: 3 (S003, S008, S012). Confidence: high.

11. **Make clear early what the lead wants.** A goal and a desire make readers root for the lead; a late call to adventure loses them. "if he delays his call to adventure, it really does not engage the audience" (S003) Sources: 3 (S003, S012, S016). Confidence: high.

12. **Use analysis to feed the creative work, not to replace it.** Fill the top of the one-page map, not every box; you do not need the controlling idea before a first draft. "you’re using analytical tools from the start to inspire you creatively. There’s a difference." (S008) "do you need to know it when you begin writing? No, I don't think so." (S003) Sources: 3 (S001, S003, S008). Confidence: high.

13. **Escalate: each complication bigger than the last, and save the biggest events.** Vary the size of the threat; hold a second huge event for an act climax rather than the opening. "If you have the same phere in every unit of story, it’s boring too." (S016) "Save that death of the captain for much later on." (S012) Sources: 3 (S003, S012, S016). Confidence: high.

14. **Self-validate, be patient, and ship.** Take sustenance from the labor, not the result, and do not chase lists. "The labor is really where you need to get your sustenance, not the results of the labor." (S019) "it’s really important that you ship and that you do put your work out there." (S020) Sources: 3 (S019, S020, S024). Confidence: high.

15. **Give a book a fair test and find its core readers first.** Get it to 10,000 readers before judging it; promote over the long haul, not on a launch date; walk up from your core market. "if you don’t get to 10,000 you will never know whether or not something can become a perennial bestseller." (S023) "There’s no such thing as a pub date, a launch date." (S001) Sources: 3 (S001, S023, S024). Confidence: high.

16. **Start in the middle and cut what only moves people around.** Open in the middle of things, trust the reader, and cut exposition and shoe leather. "A major thing that first-time writers do is they over explain, and they think that the reader isn’t going to understand unless they give them a lot of exposition." (S012) "We don’t want to see the shoe leather of a recruit coming in to talk to the boss." (S008) Sources: 2 (S008, S012). Confidence: medium.

17. **Choose the storyteller deliberately and do not switch without reason.** "You have to think about your point of view when you’re starting your story." (S012) Sources: 2 (S002, S012). Confidence: medium.

18. **Make the promise to the reader clear and early.** In fiction the genre is the promise; in big idea nonfiction state the idea and the destination up front. "The most difficult thing to do is to be very clear about your intentions of your book" (S023) "A book works when fans of the genre the book is in find it satisfying." (S002) Sources: 2 (S002, S023). Confidence: medium.

19. **When taking notes, listen and do not argue; get feedback from people who know the genre.** "Don’t explain or argue. Listen and give yourself time to metabolize before you react." (S012) He faults workshops that tear work apart with no method (S019). Sources: 2 (S012, S019). Confidence: medium.

20. **Push the core value to the end of the line only at the global level.** Breakouts take the genre's value past the negative; scenes pushed that far turn melodramatic. "if you do, it’s going to seem melodramatic; it’s going to seem like a soap opera" (S001) Sources: 1 (S001). Confidence: low.

## Views that changed over time

- **Whether every writer should go independent.** Older view, as he recalls it in 2015: he thought the right argument would convert anyone. "I used to think that if I made the right arguments to people" (S001) Newer (2014 and 2015): it suits a certain temperament only. "that’s not appealing to everybody, but it is to a certain kind of temperament" (S024) "It’s not for everybody: if you like it, great; if you get nothing out of it, throw it away!" (S001)
- **The top rule of the method.** 2015 to 2017: the value shift and the six questions are the core (S001, S002, S012). 2019: a golden rule of phere sits above the five commandments (S016). 2022, newer: the five commandments are taught through a causal and coincidental taxonomy of inciting incidents and active and revelatory turning points, without the phere term (S007). Inferred from S001, S016, S007.
- **The controlling idea.** 2019: a one-line value-triumphs-when sentence (S003). 2022, newer: at the universal level, a pattern of behavior for choosing between two colliding values (S006).
