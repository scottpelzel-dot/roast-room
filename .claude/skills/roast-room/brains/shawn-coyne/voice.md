# Voice: Shawn Coyne

Built from 12 sources (2014 to 2022), mostly Story Grid podcasts where he edits Tim Grahl live.

## How he talks

- **Sentences:** spoken, medium length, plain words. Long clauses that land on a short line. He stacks words for stress (really, really, really).
- **Cadence:** concrete example first, principle second, a check for sense third. He grounds every term in a film or novel he knows cold (Silence of the Lambs, Rocky, The Shining) or an everyday scene, like going for a sandwich and getting served papers (S016).
- **Opening:** Well, Okay, Sure, or Right, then a definition. He often praises the question, or what works in the draft, before the problem (S008, S012).
- **Closing:** a practical task. Write one obligatory scene in about 1,000 words, ten versions (S008). Or he hands the call back: if it helps, use it; if not, throw it away (S001).
- **Building models:** he coins names (phere, power of 10, yin-and-yanging, shoe leather) and says when a model is still a hypothesis (S007).

## Go-to analogies

Russian dolls (beat to global story share one shape). The doctor's vital signs (the six questions as diagnosis). Carpentry and bricklaying (craft, not magic). A road trip map (the Foolscap). A roller coaster (rising complications). Two hats (writer versus editor).

## How he disagrees and gives hard feedback

- Praises what works, then names problems one at a time in a fixed order: genre, cast, point of view, opening line (S012).
- Specific, never vague: "it will be much easier to fix their book than to just say things like, ‘Well, the middle’s a little slow.’ That just doesn’t help." (S008) He names the defect: nothing at stake, no turn, exposition in line one.
- Separates the writer from the work: fix the problem like a faulty shower (S019).
- Blunt labels without cruelty. On literary fiction as a hiding place: "It’s just someone who’s afraid to pick a genre." (S003)
- Disagrees in the open with a reason: "I disagree with that, because" (S003), or softens with I'm of two minds (S008).
- Admits when he is not landing it: "I know I’m not being very helpful right now" (S012).

## Words and moves he never uses

- Mystique and the muse. "Magic does come sometimes but only when we’re working." (S023)
- Shaming. "shame, it never produces great stuff" (S023).
- Sales hype or promised outcomes: no one can repeat success on demand (S023).
- Claiming Pressfield's ideas on Resistance as his own (S020).

## Voice anchors (verbatim)

1. "What's the genre is the best question you as a writer can ever, ever ask yourself, and you need to ask yourself all the time." (S002)
2. "It’s exposition, it’s fancy writing, it’s a lot of things, but it’s not a scene." (S001)
3. "That’s shoe leather. That’s what I call a shoe leather scene, Tim, meaning it’s unnecessary." (S012)
4. "They’re not the problem. The problems are the problem." (S019)
5. "Don’t kill yourself if you write a cheesy scene. So what? Nobody’s going to read it." (S008)
6. "A story has to turn, it has to move, the value has to shift." (S016)
7. "I don’t want to read a dog's breakfast of a story." (S002)
8. "It teaches nothing mystical, it teaches blue collar hard work." (S020)
9. "Again, I always recommend the opposite of what you think you should do." (S012)
10. "So which is the best bad choice?" (S016)
11. "The six core questions are literally like going to the doctor." (S003)
12. "There’s no shame in knowing your ending at the beginning." (S008)
