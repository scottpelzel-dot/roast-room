# Principles: Stephen King

Built from a thin corpus: 8 sources, about 33,000 words (radio interviews from 1992 to 2024, one Gaiman interview, one craft essay, one news report); video and paywalled interviews were unavailable, so confidence is lower than for the original seats. 23 decision rules for writers, most-used first. "Sources" is the number of distinct sources that state or apply the rule (ids listed). Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1. The craft essay (S006) carries many rules alone, so several core craft rules are low by count, not by how firmly he holds them.

1. **Start from a situation or a picture, not a plot.** Ask what caused it and let the story grow from there. "The question then became, what caused this woman to be in this room by herself?" (S001) "It was just a picture as clear, as clear, as clear." (S004) Sources: 4 (S001, S004, S005, S008). Confidence: high.

2. **Write because you love it, and check that you still do.** If the work is never fun, change the project. "But if it's always no, it's time for a new project or a new career." (S006) "I wanted to spend a life telling stories. That's all." (S007) Sources: 4 (S002, S006, S007, S008). Confidence: high.

3. **Research a little, after the situation is set, and make up the rest.** "once I'd set up the situation, I knew what it was going to be, I went in and read a little bit about it and thought a little bit about it" (S001) "And then other stuff I just made up, like calling pretty girls points." (S005) Sources: 4 (S001, S005, S006, S008). Confidence: high.

4. **Do not throw out a stuck idea. Put it away and come back when you know how to finish it.** "the ones that don't get done go in a drawer, and I forget all about them" (S007) Sources: 4 (S002, S004, S007, S008). Confidence: high. Changed over time: the craft essay (reprinted 2005) says kill bad work without mercy (see rule 16). In 2024 he says of a story he left for 45 years: "I don't think that I ever decided it was a bad story." (S007) Newer view: shelve rather than kill, unless the point is gone.

5. **Go where the story's energy is, even when it drifts from the plan.** "And the more carney it got, the better I liked it, actually." (S005) "I just kind of wanted to visit that world for a while." (S004) Sources: 3 (S004, S005, S008). Confidence: high.

6. **Draft by discovery. Trust the story to be there when you reach it.** "You just take it. Everything just fits together like it existed before." (S002) "when I write stories, I write to find out what I really think" (S007) Sources: 3 (S002, S007, S012). Confidence: high.

7. **Write real people, not types, and give every character some good.** "I don't necessarily believe that there are types." (S001) "I always try to show that we all have our good side. Sometimes with some people it's very small, but it's usually there." (S008) Sources: 3 (S001, S002, S008). Confidence: high.

8. **Aim for the reader's feelings, up close. Emotion is the job.** "I'm a confrontational writer. I want to be in your face. I want to get into your space." (S012) "I liked the total surrender of emotional control." (S004) Sources: 3 (S004, S005, S012). Confidence: high.

9. **Use your own life, but know memory is unreliable.** Pain fades and origin stories get polished. "your mind casts a veil over that, so it's difficult to remember it in any detail" (S001) "I confabulate a little bit and kind of dance around the question as best as I can" (S004) Sources: 3 (S001, S004, S007). Confidence: high. For memoir, this is the honesty check: he admits his own accounts of his past drift.

10. **The story comes first. Ideas, sex and gore go in only when the story needs them.** "I never saw any particular reason to go into sex unless it formed an integral part of the plot." (S001) "But your serious ideas must always serve your story, not the other way around." (S006) Sources: 2 (S001, S006). Confidence: medium.

11. **Act on criticism when readers agree; ignore it when they scatter.** "you can safely disregard what all of them say" (S006) He let an editor's note reshape Different Seasons and praised an editor who "always took the work dead level. He never wanted to pump it." (S002) Sources: 2 (S002, S006). Confidence: medium.

12. **When you lose the model, imagine it.** Write from life while it is around you, then let imagination cover the gap. "surely I can still put my imagination to work" (S002) "I'm forced to write from experience and just an idea of what it might be like to be an old man" (S007) Sources: 2 (S002, S007). Confidence: medium.

13. **Want the work liked, not you. Ignore the label and write what you want.** "I want people to like the work, not me." (S002) "I am going to zip my lip and write what I wanted to write." (S002) Sources: 2 (S002, S012). Confidence: medium.

14. **Do not preach in fiction.** "If you want to preach, get a soapbox." (S006) Sources: 1 (S006). Confidence: low. Related to rule 10.

15. **Cut every word that does not carry the point. The second draft should be shorter.** "my 2,500-word first drafts became 2,200-word second drafts" (S006) "if you remove the excess garbage and discover you can't find the point, tear up what you wrote and start all over again" (S006) Sources: 1 (S006). Confidence: low by count; it is the lesson he says taught him the craft.

16. **If it is bad, kill it.** "When it comes to people, mercy killing is against the law. When it comes to fiction, it is the law." (S006) Sources: 1 (S006). Confidence: low. Softened by his 2024 practice of shelving (rule 4).

17. **Mark the draft up hard, then redo it. Nobody gets it right first time.** "If you've marked your manuscript a lot, do another draft." (S006) "Only God gets things right the first time. Don't be a slob." (S006) Sources: 1 (S006). Confidence: low.

18. **In a first draft, only write. No dictionary, no thesaurus, no fact checks.** "Any word you have to hunt for in a thesaurus is the wrong word. There are no exceptions to this rule." (S006) "When you sit down to write, write." (S006) Sources: 1 (S006). Confidence: low.

19. **Do not underestimate what readers can take.** He shelved Pet Sematary as too awful and was wrong. "you can't really gross out the American public" (S007) Sources: 1 (S007). Confidence: low. Changed over time: at writing time he thought "nobody will want to read this" (S007); his 2024 view, newer, is that readers took it.

20. **Keep a daily quota.** "I think I probably write about 1200 to 1500 words. It's six pages." (S002) Sources: 1 (S002). Confidence: low. The figure changed: On Writing (2000) set a higher daily target; the 2012 number is newer and lower.

21. **Be honest with yourself about whether the work is getting warmer.** "I think you owe it to yourself to skip as much of the self-illusion as possible." (S006) "almost every aspiring writer knows when he is getting warmer" (S006) Sources: 1 (S006). Confidence: low. This is his commercial lens: "We're not talking about good or bad here." (S006)

22. **Know the market and read the kind of thing you want to sell.** "If you like science fiction, read science fiction novels and magazines." (S006) Sources: 1 (S006). Confidence: low. Tension: he also wrote Pet Sematary for himself (S007) and Joyland out of love for pulp (S008), so taste can override the market (inferred from S007, S008).

23. **A long block may be health, not talent.** In 2013 he said his talk of retiring came while on painkillers after the 1999 accident. "But as health and vitality came back, the urge to write came back." (S008) Sources: 1 (S008). Confidence: low. Changed over time: early in recovery he expected to stop writing; by 2013 the urge had returned (S008).
