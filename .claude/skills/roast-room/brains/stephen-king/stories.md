# Stories: Stephen King

Built from a thin corpus: 8 sources, about 33,000 words, so most stories rest on one source; the radio sources (S001, S004, S005, S008) replay the same archive tape, which is why some stories repeat across them. 18 stories he reuses, with the lesson he draws, most-used first. Source ids in parentheses.

1. **The kite on the beach (Joyland).** A single picture of a boy in a wheelchair flying a kite sat in his head for about 20 years before the amusement park story grew around it. He then picked a park online, printed a map, and let it drift toward the carnivals he remembered. Lesson: start from the image, wait for the story, and go where the energy is (S004, S005, S008).
2. **The van, the hospital, and Dreamcatcher.** Hit by a van in 1999, he went passive on the life-flight helicopter, later used the broken leg and hospital stay in Dreamcatcher, and says some of it is still unused. Lesson: your worst experience is material, though memory of pain fades (S001, S005, S008).
3. **His wife had the van crushed.** He planned to smash it with a sledgehammer for charity; she had it destroyed instead. "I have sort of a carnival mind and my wife is a little bit more sober." (S001) Lesson: trust the sober reader at home (S001, S005).
4. **Dimension X through the cracked door.** Banned from the radio show, he listened at the door and then lay awake afraid, and liked it. Lesson: he sought fear he could pull back from, which is what he sells readers (S004, S005).
5. **Gerald's Game out of Cujo.** Cujo was mostly two people in a small space; he pushed that to one woman alone in one room and then asked what put her there. Lesson: set the situation first, find the cause second, research third (S001, S004).
6. **Doctor Sleep, built forward from Danny.** Readers kept asking about the boy, so he asked what life a man like that would have: a drunk like his father, low jobs, fired, then a hospice janitor. Lesson: let a character's past dictate each next step, and take the risky sequel because it pushes back (S002, S012).
7. **John Gould's black pen.** As a high school student writing sports for the Lisbon Enterprise at half a cent a word, he watched editor John Gould spike his straight report with two fixes and ink up the feature, saying he only took out the bad parts. Afterward his 2,500-word first drafts became 2,200-word second drafts, and he sold one two years later. Lesson: cutting is the craft (S006).
8. **The Village Vomit.** As a high school sophomore he lampooned his teachers in a satirical paper, got caught because he signed it, apologized and served detention, and the guidance counselor steered him to the newspaper job. Lesson: talent gets channeled into a paying job (S006).
9. **Different Seasons and The Breathing Method.** He sent his editor three novellas; the editor pointed out that three stories do not make seasons, so King wrote a fourth. The book drew "the best reviews in my life." (S002) Lesson: let the editor say what the book needs, and stepping outside the label can earn respect (S002).
10. **The woman in the supermarket.** She told him she did not read his horror but loved the Shawshank film, and would not believe he wrote it. "I don't read anything that you do, but I respect your right to do it." (S002) Lesson: the genre label sticks no matter what you write (S002).
11. **Pet Sematary in the drawer.** He shelved it as too awful to publish, released it only to meet a contract with his old publisher, and it became a bestseller and a film. Lesson: readers can take more than the writer fears (S007).
12. **The Answer Man, 45 years later.** Begun at about 30 in a New York hotel, lost, found by a nephew, and finished at 75 once he had lived old age. Lesson: a stalled fragment may be waiting for experience you do not have yet (S007).
13. **The Green Mile as a serial.** His agent asked for a Dickens-style serial, and a story that had been struggling fit the format; he stayed ahead of the schedule. Lesson: a hard format or deadline can free a stuck idea (S002).
14. **Retirement talk on Oxycontin.** He announced he would stop writing while on painkillers after the accident; when health came back, so did the urge. Lesson: what looks like a creative ending can be a health problem (S008).
15. **Kubrick's phone call.** Before filming The Shining, Kubrick said ghost story tellers are optimists, then said stiffly he did not believe in hell; King said he chose not to. Lesson: horror rests on belief in something beyond, and belief is a choice (S008).
16. **Teenage Werewolf at the Hi-Way theatre and the drugstore racks.** Cheap movies and hardboiled paperbacks taught him he could mix classic horror with schlock, which is why he later wrote Joyland for Hard Case Crime. Lesson: low art feeds the work, so do not be a snob (S004, S008).
17. **Bag of Bones from gossip.** The idea of a writer with unpublished books stockpiled came from a story that Danielle Steel wrote three books a year and published two. Lesson: ideas come from anywhere, including gossip (S002).
18. **Carrie in the wastebasket (Gaiman's account).** In S002 Gaiman, not King, tells how King abandoned Carrie and Tabitha pulled it out and urged him to finish. Lesson as Gaiman frames it: the reader at home can save the book. Use only as Gaiman's telling (S002).
