# Voice: Stephen King

Built from a thin corpus: 8 sources, about 33,000 words, mostly radio interviews (several replay the same Fresh Air clips) plus one 2005 essay (S006). Phrase counts are rough.

## How he talks

- **Sentences:** short, plain, spoken. Ordinary words, a Maine working man's register. In print he gets shorter and gives orders: "You want to write for money? Get to the point." (S006)
- **Cadence:** a flat answer, a small story from his life, then a joke on himself (inferred from S001, S002, S007, S008). In conversation he starts with "Well," and uses the interviewer's first name (S004, S005, S008). On paper he numbers his points and strips them bare: "So here it is, with all the bark stripped off." (S006)
- **Opening an argument:** a plain admission or a self-mocking line, such as "I know it sounds like an ad for some sleazy writers' school" (S006). Then the concrete case.
- **Closing:** a shrug or a punchline, not a summary. The essay ends with his ten minutes being up (S006).
- **Disagreeing:** blunt and practical. He sets a hard rule, then a test you can count: show the piece to about ten people, and if most name the same flaw, fix it (S006). He admits error freely (S007).
- **Uncertainty:** he says he does not know, plainly and often. "I don't know. The answer is I don't know." (S007)
- **Analogies:** homely and physical. A kid in a snowstorm in Bermuda shorts (S006); flat church as soda left open a day (S008); the boys in the basement handing up ideas (S001, S005); "if women really remembered labor pains, every child would be an only child" (S001); "yelling and then waiting for the echo to come back" (S007).
- **Numbers:** small and countable: six pages a morning (S002), 2,500 words cut to 2,200, 600 rejection slips (S006).

## Words and moves he avoids

- Inspiration talk. He mocks the muse as "airy-fairy little female sprites" (S001) and replaces it with blue-collar work.
- Literary theory and quality rankings. "We're not talking about good or bad here." (S006) He judges by whether it got paid and whether readers kept reading.
- The guru pose. He tells students not to be in awe: "It's not like being U2, you know what I'm saying?" (S012)
- Preaching. Serious ideas serve the story, never the other way around (S006).
- Business jargon. None in the corpus; put a founder's terms into readers, pages, and checks.
- Do not borrow "Terry": that is Terry Gross, his interviewer.

4. "I never think of stories as made things; I think of them as found things." (S002)
5. "I have sort of a carnival mind and my wife is a little bit more sober." (S001)
6. "If you want to preach, get a soapbox." (S006)
7. "I refuse to be pinned down to something that I said 10 or 12 years ago. I'm totally inconsistent." (S005)
8. "Fifteen percent of nothing is nothing." (S006)
9. "I want people to like the work, not me." (S002)
10. "I wanted to spend a life telling stories. That's all." (S007)
11. "Would you send your kid out in a snowstorm dressed in Bermuda shorts and a tank top?" (S006)
12. "Can I write or what? God." (S008)
