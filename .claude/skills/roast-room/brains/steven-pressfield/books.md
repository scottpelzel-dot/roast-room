# Books: Steven Pressfield

Built from 9 interviews and posts (S001 to S010) via the extract notes. Book text was not fetched; every summary is in my own words, from what he and his hosts say about the books in the corpus. Years are first publication. Where a book's ideas are not in the corpus, the entry says so.

## Books on the writing life and craft

### The War of Art (2002)
- **Core thesis:** Every creative person faces Resistance, a self-generated force that tries to stop any move to a higher level. You beat it not by feeling better but by turning pro, and the Muse rewards the one who shows up.
- **Key frameworks:** Resistance and its many disguises; the amateur versus the professional; the Muse and the two planes; the one-person corporation. He wrote it from advice he gave friends who said they had a book in them and never wrote it. He thought it was only for writers; his editor Shawn Coyne saw it was for all artists, and it later found entrepreneurs too. Coyne gave it its title. It took about ten years to catch on by word of mouth. He calls the reader the hero of the book.
- **Taught in sources:** S001, S002, S003, S007, S008, S009.

### Do the Work (2011)
- **Core thesis:** A field manual for a project in progress: the points where Resistance predictably strikes from start to finish, and what to do at each.
- **Key frameworks:** As reflected in the corpus: start at the end and know your climax first; three-act structure; research as a hiding place; get the first draft down without judging it; the predictable stall points. The corpus has no long discussion of the book itself; these ideas appear in his interviews.
- **Taught in sources:** described on his site (S010) and named by hosts (S003, S009); its ideas appear in S004, S008, S009.

### Turning Pro (2012)
- **Core thesis:** The gap between amateur and pro is habits, and turning pro is a free decision that changes how you live. It has a social cost: you may leave people behind.
- **Key frameworks:** Amateur habits versus pro habits; the pro shows up, plays hurt, does not take success or failure personally, and validates himself; the shadow career (the safe job next to the real calling).
- **Taught in sources:** S001 (shadow career), S003, S007, S008, S009.

### Nobody Wants to Read Your Sh*t (2016)
- **Core thesis:** The lesson from his advertising years: no reader wants your work, so it must be so good they have no choice. Each of his careers (advertising, screenwriting, fiction, nonfiction, self-help) taught a piece of story craft.
- **Key frameworks:** In the corpus: reading as a transaction for time and attention; the hook; leading and seducing the reader; cutting hard; theme as the case the story makes; hero as theme and villain as counter-theme; genre conventions; using your own life with distance. The book also teaches concept (the big idea that frames the whole story) and a one-page plan of the story's shape. Those two tools are not discussed in the corpus, so the council should describe them only in general terms and not attribute details to him.
- **Taught in sources:** S001, S009 (the advertising lesson); S010 (site blurb); S006 (host); the craft ideas it shares are in S006, S008, S010.

### The Authentic Swing (2013)
- **Core thesis:** A short account of writing his first published novel, The Legend of Bagger Vance, after three failed attempts.
- **Key frameworks:** Not discussed in the corpus beyond his site's description.
- **Taught in sources:** S010 (site blurb).

### The Artist's Journey (2018)
- **Core thesis:** Life has two halves. The hero's journey is the search for who you are and your calling, often through a wilderness. The artist's journey is producing the work you were running from, and asking what gift you are here to give.
- **Key frameworks:** Hero's journey, then artist's journey; the passage in the wilderness that starts with denying a calling; the suppressed calling running into a malignant channel; learning to handle the voltage once the creative flow opens. He later found the same idea in Richard Rohr's Falling Upward.
- **Taught in sources:** S001, S004, S009.

## Novels

### The Legend of Bagger Vance (1995)
- **Core thesis:** A golf novel built on the Bhagavad Gita. His first published novel, at about 52 to 54 by his own varying counts.
- **What it shows the council:** His Hollywood agent dropped him for writing a book instead of a screenplay; it sold fast and became a film. It came easily after years of failure, and it began as an idea he thought was among the dumbest he ever had. Lesson he draws: follow the idea that seizes you, and an old seed can be dormant, not dead.
- **Taught in sources:** S001, S003, S004, S007, S008.

### Gates of Fire (1998)
- **Core thesis:** A novel of the 300 Spartans at Thermopylae. His biggest success.
- **What it shows the council:** The draft ran about 800 pages and the book under 400; the book came easily, the cutting was the technical work. It took about ten more years to find its audience. It also began as an idea he thought was dumb.
- **Taught in sources:** S001, S002, S004, S007, S008, S009.

### Tides of War (2000) and The Virtues of War (2004)
- **Core thesis:** Novels of the ancient Greek world, the second about Alexander the Great.
- **What it shows the council:** Telamon, a mercenary who later got his own books, walked into both unplanned and fully formed. Lesson: let characters arrive and tell you who they are.
- **Taught in sources:** S002, S003.

### The Knowledge (2013)
- **Core thesis:** A novel he calls too close to true, drawn from his 1970s New York years driving a cab and failing at his third novel. The villain is Resistance inside the hero's head; it is the origin story of The War of Art.
- **What it shows the council:** Writing from your life: it broke his vow never to write about himself; a whole book can exist to carry two true paragraphs; his real cat became a character who stands for the Muse, and Coyne's fix was to have it kidnapped.
- **Taught in sources:** S002, S003, S006.

### Thirty-six Righteous Men (2019)
- **Core thesis:** A thriller that took about three years to write.
- **What it shows the council:** It came out with no promotion and vanished; he concluded the problem was awareness, not the book, and changed how he launches books.
- **Taught in sources:** S002.

### A Man at Arms (2021)
- **Core thesis:** A historical thriller with Telamon in the first century, around Paul's letter to Corinth. The soul-level change at the climax rests on faith.
- **What it shows the council:** Years of outlines that did not catch fire until he added a young mute girl; then it came fast. He spent months promoting it full time and still did not know if it would work.
- **Taught in sources:** S001, S002, S008, S009.

### The Arcadian (announced)
- **Core thesis:** Telamon's final life, a soldier who has lived life after life; different levels of reality are part of the story. Announced in S009 (October 2025) as due the following June.
- **Taught in sources:** S009.

## Nonfiction and memoir

### The Lion's Gate (2014)
- **Core thesis:** A history of the Six-Day War told through Israeli veterans, built from about 400 hours of interviews with some 67 people.
- **What it shows the council:** Non-fiction must follow the rules of storytelling: pick a few people and follow them. He avoided the book for years because it would make him face his own identity, and he wrote it for that reason. It is also where he met the fighter pilots who rehearse every failure before a mission.
- **Taught in sources:** S001, S006, S007.

### Govt Cheese (2022)
- **Core thesis:** A memoir of his years before his first publication, a writer's road from can't to can, with chapters named after his mentors.
- **What it shows the council:** Memoir craft is selection, point of view, and voice; Resistance was loudest when writing about his own life; he missed having an editor on it. Its central lesson is that breakthroughs take years to pay off.
- **Taught in sources:** S004, S009.

## Books by others that he points writers to
- **The Story Grid (Shawn Coyne):** genre conventions, obligatory scenes, and the editor's mindset (S001).
- **The Creative Habit (Twyla Tharp):** routine and ritual (S001, S008).
- **The Game of Numbers (Nick Murray):** volume over self-judgment, which he reads as a book about Resistance (S008).
- **The Odyssey (T. E. Lawrence's translation):** the source of the invocation of the Muse he says each morning (S007, S009).
- **Falling Upward (Richard Rohr) and Memories, Dreams, Reflections (Carl Jung):** the two halves of life, and why a single dream can matter (S001, S004).
