# Positions: Steven Pressfield

Built from the extract notes on 9 sources (S001 to S010, no S005), 2014-06 to 2025-10. 25 writer topics, most-used first. Dates are interview or post months; see `sources.md`. Where his view moved, both versions sit side by side with dates and the newer one is marked. Topics with no evidence say so.

## The Muse
- **Stance:** Ideas come from a plane above us. His job is to serve the Muse, open the pipe, and get out of the way, not to chase the market. He cannot prove any of it and says so.
- **Quotes:**
  - "I consider myself a servant of the muse, and I'm just trying to be true to her and bring her stuff down to earth as best I can, as it comes through me." (S007, 2014-06)
  - "It's the unconscious, it's the goddess inspiring us, it's things we can't explain." (S003, 2017-02)
  - "And that idea has a life of its own. It wants to be born. And if we don't let it be born, we'll pay the price one way or another." (S004, 2022-12)
  - "I can't prove that that's doing any good." (S004, 2022-12)
- **Over time:** In 2017 he called it the unconscious (S003). Newer, 2025-10: "And I don't think it's the subconscious. It's our job to open the pipeline and get out of the way." (S009)
- **Ritual:** he says the invocation from the Odyssey (T. E. Lawrence's translation) out loud every morning before work. "every morning, before I sit down to work, I say that prayer, you know, out loud and in full earnest." (S009, 2025-10; also S007)
- **Sources:** S001, S002, S003, S004, S006, S007, S008, S009 (8).

## Resistance
- **Stance:** Resistance is a self-generated force that rises whenever you try to move from a lower level to a higher one. It talks in your voice, it is strongest against the work that matters most, and it never goes away. Its only power is your fear of it.
- **Quotes:**
  - "When in doubt, it's resistance." (S003, 2017-02)
  - "I consider it's all self-generated. I don't think it comes from out there." (S007, 2014-06)
  - "it really has no strength of its own. The only strength that it has is our fear of it." (S008, 2021-04)
  - "The bigger the tree, the bigger the shadow." (S009, 2025-10)
  - "the dragon has to be slain every morning anew." (S008, 2021-04)
- **Forms it takes:** perfectionism ("make you such a perfectionist that you spend all day on one paragraph and you accomplish nothing." S001), research ("Cut to a year later, how many words do you have on paper? None." S008), and the people closest to you ("a lot of times those voices will be the voices closest to us." S009).
- **Not everything is Resistance:** fussing over character names is craft. "So I don't think that is resistance." (S004, 2022-12) He also doubted that his years of avoiding The Lion's Gate were Resistance (S007).
- **Over time:** he used to think it hit only the writing. Newer, 2021: it hits marketing too. "Oh my God, it also happens when you're trying to market it." (S008, 2021-04; also S002, 2021-03)
- **Limit:** "And why it's there? I don't know. Why did God put it there? I don't know." (S008)
- **Sources:** S001, S002, S003, S004, S007, S008, S009 (7).

## Amateur versus professional
- **Stance:** The difference is habits, not talent or publication. Turning pro is a free decision, and then the whole way you live changes. A pro shows up every day, plays hurt, and does not consult feelings. Act as a pro before anyone pays you.
- **Quotes:**
  - "A pro shows up every day. A pro stays on the job every day. A pro plays hurt." (S003, 2017-02)
  - "I was an amateur, and I had amateur habits, and I thought like an amateur." (S007, 2014-06)
  - "a professional doesn't care how he or she feels. That's irrelevant in the professional's mind." (S008, 2021-04)
  - "You have to operate as a full-bore professional, or you'll never get to that level." (S003, 2017-02)
  - "You're a professional person. Do it. Whatever it takes. Do it." (S004, 2022-12; his restatement of what Hugh Reeves taught him)
- **Why the frame:** it removes blame. "it takes the value judgment out of it" (S003)
- **Cost:** "turning pro does have a cost. So a lot of times, if you take that course, you have to leave people behind." (S009, 2025-10)
- **Sources:** S001, S002, S003, S004, S007, S008, S009 (7).

## Fear and self-doubt
- **Stance:** The voice that says you are an idiot, the idea is done, or you have lost it is Resistance, not you. Name it, dismiss it by will, and step into the fear. Every project starts with months of doubt, and it passes.
- **Quotes:**
  - "we hear these voices in our head, self-doubt, and we think those are our thoughts. But they're not our thoughts. That's Resistance." (S007, 2014-06)
  - "There's nothing wrong with you. You haven't lost any pixie dust. That is pure Resistance." (S001, 2021-02)
  - "That was just a period of doubt. That's all it is. That was just Resistance, and I just had to push through it." (S002, 2021-03)
  - "once we step into that fear, it's like, it goes away." (S008, 2021-04)
- **Threshold:** three or four months of doubt at the start of a project is normal. "But a lot of times, after three months or four months or something, you'll start to get traction on it." (S002)
- **Over time:** the inner critic was loud when he started. Newer, 2025-10: "It almost never comes up, the inner critic." (S009)
- **Sources:** S001, S002, S003, S004, S007, S008, S009 (7).

## Age and late success
- **Stance:** Life is long and breakthroughs pay off a decade late. Age is no excuse; at 82 he plans more books. Be patient with yourself.
- **Quotes:**
  - "I joke that I was an overnight success after 30 years." (S001, 2021-02)
  - "It's like you have a breakthrough and nothing happens. It's like 10 years later, it pays off." (S004, 2022-12)
  - "Life is long. It's longer than we think." (S009, 2025-10)
  - "I might be around for another 20 years or more. That's a career. I could write 15 books." (S009, 2025-10)
- **Facts that disagree:** he says he was "53 or 54 years old" when Bagger Vance came out (S008, 2021-04; also S001). Huberman's intro says he published his first book at 52 (S009, host's words). Elsewhere he puts the gap at about 28 to 30 years (S001) and about 30 years from quitting advertising (S008).
- **Slow books:** The War of Art took about ten years to find readers (S002, S007, S008), and Gates of Fire about another ten (S002).
- **Sources:** S001, S002, S003, S004, S007, S008, S009 (7).

## Daily routine and hours
- **Stance:** Same thing, same time, every day. Habit is the main weapon against Resistance. Two hours a day is a full writing day. Dive in without warming up, stop when the typos start, and do not reread the day's pages.
- **Quotes:**
  - "And it's habit and habit is a mighty ally in the war against resistance." (S008, 2021-04)
  - "if you can squeeze out a couple of hours a day, you're on the same level with me, the same level with a full-time writer." (S009, 2025-10)
  - "But I stop when I start making mistakes, when I start having typos and things like that." (S009, 2025-10)
  - "Did I put in the time, and did I work as hard as I can?" (S009, 2025-10)
- **Over time:** he used to write four hours. Newer, 2025-10: "I can do in two hours now what I used to do in four." (S009) In 2021 Ferriss read out an older interview giving two and a half hours as his outer limit; he did not correct it (S001, host-read, not his words here).
- **Not one size:** "And I don't think there's any kind of one size fits all type of thing." (S001) Eight-hour days like Joyce Carol Oates's are "beyond my comprehension." (S001)
- **Little successes:** a string of small wins from waking (the gym first) builds momentum to the desk (S001, S009).
- **Sources:** S001, S002, S003, S007, S008, S009 (6).

## Rejection and failure
- **Stance:** Failure is most of life. Name the mistake, fix it, and do not conclude you are a loser. Do not study a failure's reception. Tell failure stories; they help readers more than success stories.
- **Quotes:**
  - "Okay, I crashed and burned, but that doesn't mean that I'm a loser. I just did something wrong. I'll correct it." (S003, 2017-02)
  - "Most of life, I think, is failure, and just getting up and trying again." (S002, 2021-03)
  - "I couldn't sell that book. I couldn't sell the next book. It was years and years before I could." (S004, 2022-12)
  - "One of the attributes of a professional, I think, is that they don't take success or failure personally." (S003, 2017-02)
- **His record:** three unpublished novels and about 13 years of failed novel writing (S001, S003), then six or seven years in Hollywood before he earned a penny (S003), and the King Kong Lives flop (S008, S009).
- **Sources:** S001, S002, S003, S004, S008, S009 (6).

## The day job and money
- **Stance:** A day job does not stop you; bring its discipline to the writing. Two hours a day beside a full-time job is enough. He took manual and advertising jobs and had no backup plan.
- **Quotes:**
  - "In our day jobs, we show up every day, whether we want to or not." (S007, 2014-06)
  - "it is possible to have a full-time job and still do your artistic thing to a full-tilt version." (S009, 2025-10)
  - "I was always so depressed at the end of the day working at a real job that the only way I could pull myself out of it was to sit down at the keyboard and keep trying to write something." (S008, 2021-04)
  - "Basically I didn't have a real backup plan that worked." (S008, 2021-04)
- **Money:** "I saved $2,700 working in advertising in New York." (S001) Now: "I've had enough success to pay the rent, which is good enough for me, you know?" (S009)
- **Sources:** S001, S003, S004, S007, S008, S009 (6).

## Editors and feedback
- **Stance:** A good editor (for him, Shawn Coyne) knows the structures that work and tells you what the book is about. Learn that thinking so you can edit yourself. Otherwise, judge your own work and ignore what people say.
- **Quotes:**
  - "I'll write 10 drafts and give it to him, and then he'll tell me what it's about." (S002, 2021-03)
  - "I think we all have to get to the place where we can be a judge of our own work." (S007, 2014-06)
  - "the ideal is to not listen to anything that anybody says about what you did and to judge it only yourself, you know?" (S009, 2025-10)
  - "I wished I had a real editor, which I didn't at this point." (S004, 2022-12)
- **Family and friends as readers:** do not expect them to read or cheer; they sense you changing (S001, S003, S009).
- **Tension:** he says to judge alone, yet credits Coyne with his themes, the kidnapped cat in The Knowledge, and the title The War of Art (S001, S006, S009). Inferred from S001, S006, S009: he trusts one expert editor, not general feedback.
- **Sources:** S001, S002, S004, S006, S008, S009 (6).

## Concept
- **Stance:** Go big. Of several ideas, do the one that scares you most. When an idea seizes you, cling to it, even if it looks dumb and uncommercial. Then check for a tailwind.
- **Quotes:**
  - "if you want to know which one of three or four projects that you should do, you should do the one you're most afraid of." (S009, 2025-10)
  - "First of all, don't start small." (S001, 2021-02)
  - "I do find usually when an idea seizes me, it really seizes me. It's like falling in love with somebody" (S006, 2017-01)
  - "Any time something is brand new, it seems absurd." (S004, 2022-12)
  - "And if it doesn't feel like that, then maybe it's not the right idea." (S001, 2021-02)
- **Tension:** the fear test can mislead. "But again, Resistance with a capital R is so diabolical at fooling you, that it can fool you." (S002) He admits he has no firm answer on choosing the next project (S002).
- **Hook:** make it so good the reader cannot look away; an advertising lesson (S001, S009).
- **Sources:** S001, S002, S004, S006, S007, S009 (6).

## Story structure
- **Stance:** Three acts with planned turning points. Know the climax first and work back. The hero must change, on the soul level, and the change embodies the theme. Nonfiction obeys the same rules.
- **Quotes:**
  - "start at the end, always know what the climax is and what the final scene is, and then work backwards from that." (S008, 2021-04)
  - "I'm a believer in three act structure, act one, act two, act three. And that there's a dividing point at the end of act one." (S008, 2021-04)
  - "any hero, they have to change or there's no story" (S002, 2021-03)
  - "non-fiction is fiction, in the sense that, I don't mean that you can make anything up, but in non-fiction, you have to go by the rules of storytelling." (S007, 2014-06)
- **Where he learned it:** "Working in the movies was a real college education, PhD, in learning about the principles of storytelling" (S003). He relays Coyne's genre conventions and obligatory scenes (S001).
- **Sources:** S001, S002, S003, S007, S008, S010 (6).

## First drafts
- **Stance:** Turn off the censor, spew from A to Z, and keep moving. Go around hard spots; the Muse solves them later. Never judge the day's work and never reread it.
- **Quotes:**
  - "I will then just turn off the self sensor and just spew stuff. And my goal is just to get from page one to the end, no matter how crappy it is" (S008, 2021-04)
  - "if you encounter an obstacle, go around it. Leave it behind, even if it will threaten your supply lines and stuff like that." (S004, 2022-12)
  - "Momentum I think is everything in a case like that." (S004, 2022-12)
  - "At the end of the day, I never read what I wrote." (S009, 2025-10)
- **Self-censorship:** "You've got to get rid of that: that's an amateur habit." (S007)
- **Sources:** S001, S004, S007, S008, S009 (5).

## Revision and how many drafts
- **Stance:** Plan for many drafts and fix one thing per draft. Think like an editor in the middle drafts. In late drafts, the first move when stuck is to cut; readers fill the gaps.
- **Quotes:**
  - "And you can only fix so much in one draft. You can only fix one thing in one draft." (S009, 2025-10)
  - "I do maybe 15 drafts of a book and seventh, eighth, ninth, I'm really thinking like an editor." (S001, 2021-02)
  - "A lot of times you can cut a lot and the audience will accept it or the readers will accept it." (S004, 2022-12)
- **Draft counts disagree:** about 15 (S001, 2021-02), 10 before Coyne sees it (S002, 2021-03), and "maybe 13, 14, or 15 drafts. The last 7 or 8 would be really small, really slight changes." (S009, 2025-10, newest)
- **Cutting Gates of Fire:** 802 pages to 384 (S001), or "800 pages long. And the book came out to be 375." (S008)
- **Sources:** S001, S002, S004, S008, S009 (5).

## Theme
- **Stance:** Every story needs a theme, a strong statement about life that need not be true. It hides, so ask over and over what the book is about. Once known, nothing off theme goes in, and every character carries an aspect of it.
- **Quotes:**
  - "Every story must have a theme." (S010, 2017-12)
  - "It is enough, for storytelling purposes, that a theme be a strong statement about some aspect of life or the human condition." (S010, 2017-12)
  - "I don't know why, but the theme wants to hide and keep away from us." (S006, 2017-01)
  - "One of the principles of storytelling is that every character in the book must represent an aspect of the theme." (S006, 2017-01)
- **Over time:** for 25 years "I wrote completely on instinct" (S006), and Coyne told him his themes (S001, S002). Newer, 2017 on: he starts a Theme file and tries to find it earlier, and by 2021 asks the "really about" question himself (S006, S002).
- **Titles:** "I think the key thing about a title is it has to be on theme." (S006) He finds them hard and often others title his books (S009).
- **Sources:** S001, S002, S006, S008, S010 (5).

## Memoir and writing from your own life
- **Stance:** Raw life is lame and ordinary on the page. You need distance to see yourself as a character, and you cannot write a wound that still has you. Memoir is a performance. If the truth is too close, move it into fiction or metaphor.
- **Quotes:**
  - "the real skill is being able to zoom back and see a character that's you in a completely objective way." (S006, 2017-01)
  - "And as long as that's the case, I don't think it's possible to write about it." (S006, 2017-01)
  - "You've got to be incredibly careful in using that real stuff because it goes over into self-pity." (S006, 2017-01)
  - "We have to remember it's a performance." (S006, 2017-01)
  - "How do I tell this fucking story? What do I leave out? What do I leave in? What's the point of view? What's my tone of voice?" (S004, 2022-12)
- **Over time:** after three unpublished autobiographical novels, "I vowed that from then on I would never write anything that had anything to do with me." (S006, 2017-01) He broke the vow with The Knowledge, a "too close to true" novel (S003, S006). Newer, 2022-12: he wrote a straight memoir, Govt Cheese, "Because I thought it would be helpful to other people who were on that odyssey." (S004)
- **The doubt is loudest here:** "Who cares about this, Steve? Who wants to hear about these dumb things that happened to you?" (S002)
- **Sources:** S002, S003, S004, S006, S009 (5).

## Critics
- **Stance:** Do not believe the critics' praise or their insults, and do not look to the market for validation. He admits he still feels it.
- **Quotes:**
  - "You can't look to the response of the marketplace as validation for your work, because they're going to love stuff that stinks, and they're going to hate stuff that's great." (S007, 2014-06)
  - "I'm trying not to. I'm human, and you know." (S009, 2025-10)
  - "I think it's a seductive thing that's only going to pull you in the wrong direction, you know?" (S009, 2025-10)
  - "Because they're either not going to respond at all or they're going to hate it." (S004, 2022-12)
- **Hemingway, as he relays it:** "if you believe the critics when they tell you you're great, then you have to believe them when they tell you you stink" (S007)
- **The worst review:** the King Kong Lives review mocked the writers' names (S008); "it kept you humble." (S009)
- **Sources:** S004, S006, S007, S008, S009 (5).

## What to do after finishing
- **Stance:** Never be "between books." Start the next one in the last months of this one, and on the day it ends begin the next. Do not wait for the world's response.
- **Quotes:**
  - "There should never be 'between books.'" (S004, 2022-12)
  - "As I'm coming towards, say, the last six months on a book, I want to be starting the next book simultaneously. Even if it's only notes." (S004, 2022-12)
  - "The worst thing a writer or filmmaker or anybody can do, I'm sure you know this, Tim, it's like finish something, put it out there, and then sort of wait for the world to respond." (S004, 2022-12)
  - "a professional does not take success or failure personally but keeps on going and does the next one, and the next one, and the next one." (S009, 2025-10)
- **The bigger question:** "What is book one? What is book two? What is book three? What is my gift? What am I here to give?" (S001)
- **After a success:** "it's real easy to backslide, particularly when you have a success" (S008)
- **Sources:** S001, S002, S004, S008, S009 (5).

## Finishing and shipping
- **Stance:** Finish what you start. Finishing one book breaks the story that you always quit. Drop a doubtful idea early if you must, but once deep in, do not pull the pin. Perfectionism is Resistance; when it is ready, let it go.
- **Quotes:**
  - "once I was able to finish that thing, that novel, I've never had any trouble finishing anything ever again." (S001, 2021-02)
  - "I'd rather finish it and have it fail" (S002, 2021-03)
  - "I will die before I'll give up on this project." (S009, 2025-10)
  - "that sort of perfectionism is a form of resistance and really has to be avoided at all costs." (S009, 2025-10)
  - "But when a thing is ready to go, let it go." (S009, 2025-10)
- **Limit:** "I don't know if that's a bad idea or not." (S002) He believes quitting late is bad, but as a belief, not a proven rule.
- **Sources:** S001, S002, S004, S009 (4).

## Marketing a book
- **Stance:** He hates it and does it anyway. The writer's real problem is awareness. Feeling that self-promotion is bad is amateur thinking. Expect as much time marketing as writing, and accept the result.
- **Quotes:**
  - "for a writer the real problem is, how do you gain awareness out in the real world that your book even exists, forget about whether it's good or bad." (S006, 2017-01)
  - "I just can't stand to have another book of mine come out and just vanish without a trace." (S002, 2021-03)
  - "I will have spent as much time on marketing as I did writing a book" (S002, 2021-03)
  - "I still hate to do it, I still feel bad about it, but I realized that I just have to." (S002, 2021-03)
- **Over time:** in 2014 he said indie writers need a platform: "you have to blog or do something, or speak" (S007). Newer, 2021: he does weeks of full-time promotion himself, copied from Ryan Holiday and Jack Carr (S002, S008).
- **Limit:** "I've been blitzing this thing now for months and I have no idea if it's going to work." (S002) And he admits it is harder from scratch (S002).
- **Sources:** S002, S006, S007, S008 (4).

## Publishing (traditional versus self)
- **Stance:** Traditional publishers do little for most writers now. A big book can still need a mainstream sales force; smaller books can go indie and earn ten times more per copy. Either way, act as a pro.
- **Quotes:**
  - "You only have to sell one-tenth as many books to make the same amount of money." (S007, 2014-06)
  - "for certain writers of certain books, it's a godsend" (S007, 2014-06)
  - "The book business for the writer's point of view is harder than it's ever been." (S006, 2017-01)
  - "Because, these days, if you go with mainstream publishers, they really don't do anything for you or they only do the very expected stuff, and that just doesn't work anymore." (S002, 2021-03)
- **Over time:** 2014: unsure self-publishing is the future for everyone, worried about a world with "no gatekeepers anymore." (S007) 2017: the winners "build a cult or a tribe themselves." (S006) Newer, 2021: mainstream publishers do nothing for you (S002).
- **Numbers:** 35 cents a copy from Hachette against $3.50 self-published (S007). He co-publishes with Coyne through Black Irish Books (S007, S009).
- **Limit:** he defers to Coyne on the business (S006).
- **Sources:** S002, S003, S006, S007 (4).

## Outlining and the one-page plan
- **Stance:** Block out the key scenes and the climax, then leave room for happy accidents. Some outlines never catch fire; he keeps trying until one does.
- **Quotes:**
  - "So I do try to plan it, but a lot of stuff happens along the way that are happy accidents, you hope." (S008, 2021-04)
  - "You know how you're looking for a story and you do an outline, you do another outline, it doesn't work and it doesn't catch fire." (S002, 2021-03)
  - "I sort of started with, where is he at, at the start of the story and where do I want him to be on the soul level at the end of the story?" (S002, 2021-03)
- **The one-page plan:** no recorded position. Nearest: his "Understory" outline of the hero's soul-level arc (S002) and Chayefsky's theme on one strip of paper, which he relays (S006).
- **Sources:** S001, S002, S008 (3).

## Villains
- **Stance:** The villain embodies the counter-theme and wants the opposite of the hero. The best villains cannot be reasoned with and never stop, which makes the villain a metaphor for Resistance. The hero's inner flaw echoes the outer villain.
- **Quotes:**
  - "Whatever the protagonist wants, the antagonist wants the opposite." (S010, 2017-12)
  - "The villain in Casablanca is the idea that there is such a thing as safe neutrality" (S010, 2017-12)
  - "the villain is a metaphor for resistance." (S008, 2021-04)
  - "the hero at the start cannot defeat the villain. Doesn't have the chops, doesn't have the tools, has to evolve, has to grow, has to face something." (S008, 2021-04)
- **In memoir:** the villain of The Knowledge is Resistance, inside the hero's head (S003).
- **Sources:** S003, S008, S010 (3).

## Genre
- **Stance:** Each genre has obligatory scenes and conventions; break them only with a reason. Commercially, pick one identity. He does not follow his own advice because the Muse leads him across genres.
- **Quotes:**
  - "a thriller has certain obligatory scenes and conventions" (S001, 2021-02, relaying Coyne)
  - "it's a lot smarter commercially, I think, to find an identity" (S006, 2017-01)
  - "The muse leads me in a lot of different directions." (S006, 2017-01)
  - "I just was called to it. I believe in the muse. I believe that there's something that inspires you." (S008, 2021-04, on writing the ancient world)
- **Limit:** he floats a pen name but has never used one: "I've never done it. I don't know how it works, but I think there might be something to that." (S006)
- **Sources:** S001, S006, S008 (3).

## The shadow career
- **Stance:** A job next to the real calling that carries no real risk, so failing at it means nothing. The fix is to find the real dream and take the actual track to it. He floats the diagnosis gently and admits he may be wrong.
- **Quotes:**
  - "They really want to direct or they really want to write, but for whatever reasons, they were afraid to do it." (S001, 2021-02)
  - "It may be, Tim, that writing fiction is your calling. I don't know." (S001, 2021-02)
  - "at least I was working in my craft. So I was able to keep going, because I was making a little money and I was learning." (S008, 2021-04, on his screenwriting years)
  - "we're blind to its significance while we're in it." (S004, 2022-12, on his wilderness years)
- **Note:** the line that a shadow career "entails no real risk" is from his 2012 post, read aloud by Ferriss (S001, host-read), so it is paraphrased here.
- **Inferred from S001, S004, S008:** he does not count screenwriting or manual jobs as a shadow career when they taught craft or kept him writing.
- **Sources:** S001, S004, S008 (3).

## AI
- **No recorded position.** None of the nine sources has him speaking on AI. (In S006 the host raised AI and author-name algorithms; he did not respond on it.)
