# Principles: Steven Pressfield

Built from 9 interviews and posts (S001 to S010, 2014 to 2025) via the extract notes. 23 decision rules for a writer, most-used first. "Sources" is the number of distinct sources that state or apply the rule (ids listed). Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1. A section on views that changed over time closes the file.

1. **When in doubt, it's Resistance: name the voice and do not own it.** The thought that you are not good enough, too old, or have lost it is not yours; dismiss it and keep working. "we hear these voices in our head, self-doubt, and we think those are our thoughts. But they're not our thoughts. That's Resistance." (S007) "There's nothing wrong with you. You haven't lost any pixie dust. That is pure Resistance." (S001) Sources: 6 (S001, S003, S004, S007, S008, S009). Confidence: high.

2. **Show up every day whether you feel like it or not.** Treat the work like the day job you would never skip. "In our day jobs, we show up every day, whether we want to or not." (S007) "a professional doesn't care how he or she feels. That's irrelevant in the professional's mind." (S008) Sources: 5 (S001, S003, S007, S008, S009). Confidence: high.

3. **Be patient: life is long and breakthroughs pay off years later.** His first novel came after about 30 years, and his two biggest books each took about a decade to find readers. "It's like you have a breakthrough and nothing happens. It's like 10 years later, it pays off." (S004) "Life is long. It's longer than we think." (S009) Sources: 5 (S001, S002, S004, S007, S009). Confidence: high.

4. **Once you are deep in, finish. Never pull the pin.** Drop a doubtful idea early if you must, but finishing is what breaks the habit of quitting. "I'd rather finish it and have it fail" (S002) "I will die before I'll give up on this project." (S009) Sources: 4 (S001, S002, S004, S009). Confidence: high.

5. **Do the project you are most afraid of, and go big.** Fear marks the work that matters to your growth. Check the pull over the following months, since Resistance can fool you even here. "the way you'll know the idea is that it will be terrifying to you, the prospect of exposing yourself to do this. And that's the one you should do." (S001) "But again, Resistance with a capital R is so diabolical at fooling you, that it can fool you." (S002) Sources: 4 (S001, S002, S007, S009). Confidence: high.

6. **Fix one thing per draft; in later drafts, cut.** Expect 10 to 15 drafts. "maybe 13, 14, or 15 drafts. The last 7 or 8 would be really small, really slight changes." (S009) "to find that you can say maybe in 25 words what you had said in 250 words before." (S001) Sources: 5 (S001, S002, S004, S008, S009). Confidence: high.

7. **Never judge a first draft day by day. Protect momentum.** Do not reread today's pages; judge the day on effort. "But each day I never judge myself on what I'm doing. All I want to do is keep moving" (S008) "Did I put in the time, and did I work as hard as I can?" (S009) Sources: 3 (S004, S008, S009). Confidence: high.

8. **Turn off the self-censor on the page.** Self-censorship is an amateur habit and refuses what the Muse sends. "You've got to get rid of that: that's an amateur habit." (S007) "self-censorship is an insult to the muse, to the goddess, because she's giving you something" (S007) Sources: 3 (S001, S007, S008). Confidence: high.

9. **Do not let the market, critics, or reception judge the work. Learn to judge it yourself.** "You can't look to the response of the marketplace as validation for your work, because they're going to love stuff that stinks, and they're going to hate stuff that's great." (S007) "And so, you can only ask, did you do your best?" (S009) Sources: 4 (S004, S007, S008, S009). Confidence: high.

10. **Know what the story is really about. Hero embodies the theme, villain the counter-theme.** If the story drags, this is the first fix. "Every story must have a theme." (S010) "If we can lick these problems in our story, everything else will fall into place." (S010) Sources: 4 (S001, S002, S006, S010). Confidence: high.

11. **Expect the people closest to you to resist your change, and do not take their doubts as truth.** They are fighting their own Resistance; your work is a reproach to them. Not all of them do it, and they are not bad people. "it's that they're dealing with their own unconscious resistance." (S003) "a lot of times those voices will be the voices closest to us." (S009) Sources: 3 (S001, S003, S009). Confidence: high.

12. **Write to please yourself, then make it so good the reader cannot refuse.** Do not chase or second-guess the market in choosing what to write; serve the reader in how you write it (inferred from S001, S002, S009). "But I would say, just write to please yourself, and always take the brave choice." (S001) "I'm just trying to make it as easy, and as interesting, and as fun as I can for the reader." (S009) Sources: 3 (S001, S002, S009). Confidence: high.

13. **Promotion is part of the job; feeling it makes you a bad person is amateur thinking.** He still hates it and does it anyway, and he does not know if it works. "But I know I shouldn't think that way and I know that that's a real amateur way of thinking." (S002) "for a writer the real problem is, how do you gain awareness out in the real world that your book even exists, forget about whether it's good or bad." (S006) Sources: 4 (S002, S006, S007, S008). Confidence: high.

14. **Catch ideas the moment they come; they fade.** Write down any name or idea at once, even for a book three books away. "anytime something does pop into my head, I will write it down." (S004) "And you have got to grab it while it's there." (S009) Sources: 3 (S004, S006, S009). Confidence: high.

15. **Start the next book before this one ends. Never wait on the world's response.** "The worst thing a writer or filmmaker or anybody can do, I'm sure you know this, Tim, it's like finish something, put it out there, and then sort of wait for the world to respond." (S004) Sources: 2 (S004, S009). Confidence: medium.

16. **Perfectionism is Resistance. When the work is ready, let it go.** A friend never mailed his finished novel and died with it in the box. "that sort of perfectionism is a form of resistance and really has to be avoided at all costs." (S009) "But when a thing is ready to go, let it go." (S009) Sources: 2 (S001, S009). Confidence: medium.

17. **Do not blame yourself for failing. Name the mistake and fix it.** The amateur-versus-pro frame exists to take the judgment out. "Okay, I crashed and burned, but that doesn't mean that I'm a loser. I just did something wrong. I'll correct it." (S003) "Those are really unproductive ways of looking at it. Because they're judgmental. They just make it harder for us." (S008) Sources: 2 (S003, S008). Confidence: medium.

18. **Call yourself a writer and act as a pro before anyone publishes you.** "You have to operate as a full-bore professional, or you'll never get to that level." (S003) "They can't say it because they're waiting for the world to validate them." (S008) Sources: 2 (S003, S008). Confidence: medium.

19. **Research can be a hiding place; invent what you cannot know.** "Cut to a year later, how many words do you have on paper? None. But it's a distraction, obviously." (S008) "You have to be brave and be willing to state things that you don't know for sure." (S002) Sources: 2 (S002, S008). Confidence: medium.

20. **Write your own life only once it no longer has you, and treat it as a performance.** "And as long as that's the case, I don't think it's possible to write about it." (S006) "We have to remember it's a performance." (S006) Sources: 2 (S004, S006). Confidence: medium.

21. **A day job is not an excuse: two real hours a day is enough.** Protect the session: same time, no phone or internet, stop when the mistakes start. "it is possible to have a full-time job and still do your artistic thing to a full-tilt version." (S009) "pressing forward at the end of a long day to get just a little bit more is the falsest kind of economy, because you pay for it the next day." (S009) Sources: 2 (S008, S009). Confidence: medium.

22. **Not every hesitation is Resistance. Real craft work and real dread can look like stalling.** Taking time over character names is craft, not Resistance, and he doubts his years of avoiding The Lion's Gate were Resistance. This is the check on over-using his own lens. "So I don't think that is resistance." (S004) "I really don't think it was resistance" (S007) Sources: 2 (S004, S007). Confidence: medium.

23. **After a success, guard against backsliding.** "it's real easy to backslide, particularly when you have a success" (S008) Sources: 1 (S008). Confidence: low.

## Where his views changed (newer view marked)

- **Does Resistance fade?** 2017: "you have to slay the dragon again every morning. It never goes away, never gets any easier." (S003) 2021: "Right now I'm starting a new project. I'm in the throws of resistance. It's beating the hell out of me." (S008) **Newer (2025):** "It almost never comes up, the inner critic." (S009) He still says Resistance grows with a project's importance (S009), so read this as the inner critic quieting with practice, not Resistance ending (inferred from S003, S008, S009).
- **Where Resistance strikes.** He once thought it hit only while writing. **Newer (2021):** it hits promotion just as hard. "I find that I have tremendous Resistance, with a capital R, against promoting my own stuff." (S002) (also S008)
- **Writing about himself.** 2017, looking back: "I vowed that from then on I would never write anything that had anything to do with me." (S006) **Newer (2022):** he wrote a memoir of his years before publication, to help others on the same road (S004, also S009).
- **Theme.** For his first 25 years: "I wrote completely on instinct" (S006) and his editor told him the theme (S001). **Newer (2017 to 2021):** do the theme exercise early and ask the really-about question yourself (S006, S002).
- **Daily hours.** **Newer (2025):** "What I tell myself, and I think it's true, is I can do in two hours now what I used to do in four." (S009)
- **Titles.** 2017: a title must be on theme, and locking it first is a great help, though rare (S006). 2025: he does titles last, finds them hard, and others have often titled his books (S009). Not a reversal; both are his practice at different times.
